## IFC Performance Standard 4 on community health, safety, and security

### Overview and Regulatory Context

IFC Performance Standard 4 (PS4), *Community Health, Safety, and Security*, is one of the eight Performance Standards within IFC's 2012 Sustainability Framework. PS4 recognises that project activities, equipment, and infrastructure can increase community exposure to risks and impacts. While acknowledging the public authorities' role in promoting the health, safety, and security of the public, this Performance Standard addresses the client's responsibility to avoid or minimize the risks and impacts to community health, safety, and security that may arise from project related-activities, with particular attention to vulnerable groups.

PS4's implementation is not a standalone compliance exercise: it is operationalized through the same architecture established in Performance Standard 1. The implementation of the actions necessary to meet the requirements of this Performance Standard is managed through the client's Environmental and Social Management System, the elements of which are outlined in Performance Standard 1. Where community health, safety, and security risks and impacts are identified, the client is required to manage them through its ESMS consistent with PS1.

### Objectives

PS4 sets out two principal objectives:

1. To anticipate and avoid adverse impacts on the health and safety of the Affected Community during the project life from both routine and non-routine circumstances.
2. To ensure that the safeguarding of personnel and property is carried out in accordance with relevant human rights principles and in a manner that avoids or minimizes risks to the Affected Communities.

### Scope of Application

Business activities and infrastructure projects may expose local communities to increased risks and adverse impacts related to worksite accidents, hazardous materials, spread of diseases, or interactions with private security personnel. PS4 helps companies adopt responsible practices to reduce such risks including through emergency preparedness and response, security force management, and design safety measures.

PS4 requirements build on the risk and impact identification process conducted under PS1, meaning community health, safety, and security (CHSS) considerations must be integrated from the earliest stages of the social risks and impacts identification process rather than treated as an afterthought.

### Core Requirement Areas

PS4 is organized around several substantive requirement areas that together define the CHSS risk envelope:

**Infrastructure and Equipment Design and Safety**

Clients must design, construct, operate, and decommission project components in accordance with applicable industry and national standards, considering risks and impacts to the Affected Community throughout the project life-cycle — including structural safety, fire safety, and traffic/road safety associated with project-related transport.

**Hazardous Materials Management**

Clients must avoid or minimize the potential for community exposure to hazardous materials that may be released by the project, and this requirement is cross-referenced to the World Bank Group General Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) Guidelines (for example, Section 1.5, Hazardous Materials Management).

**Ecosystem Services and Natural Resource Use**

The diminution or degradation of natural resources, such as adverse impacts on the quality, quantity, and availability of freshwater, may result in health-related risks and impacts, and where appropriate and feasible, the client will identify those risks and potential impacts on priority ecosystem services that may be exacerbated by climate change.

**Community Exposure to Disease**

Clients must avoid or minimize the potential for community exposure to water-borne, water-based, vector-borne, and communicable diseases that could result from project activities, taking into account differentiated exposure to and heightened susceptibility of vulnerable groups.

**Emergency Preparedness and Response**

Clients must prepare and implement Emergency Preparedness and Response Plans (EPRPs) that appropriately address community-facing emergency scenarios (not solely worksite/occupational scenarios), and further requirements and guidance on this subject, including some of the basic elements of emergency preparedness and response plans, are provided in Performance Standard 1 and the accompanying Guidance Note.

**Security Personnel Management**

Where the client determines that it must use security personnel to safeguard company personnel and property, security should be provided in a manner that does not jeopardize the community's safety and security, or the client's relationship with the community. This should also be consistent with national requirements, including national laws implementing host country obligations under international law, and with the requirements of Performance Standard 4, which are consistent with good international practice. This includes:

- Screening and background-checking security personnel (public and private)
- Contractual clauses binding security providers to codes of conduct on community interaction
- Rules governing the use of force, proportionate to the nature and extent of the threat
- Restrictions on the deployment of armed security personnel and requirements that, where used, personnel are properly trained
- Investigation and reporting of unlawful or abusive acts by security personnel
- Consideration of potential conflict escalation and community relations impacts of security arrangements

**Conflict and Fragile-Context Sensitivity**

The risks that a project could exacerbate an already sensitive local situation and stress scarce local resources should not be overlooked, as it may lead to further conflict — a "do no harm" / conflict-sensitivity lens that applies with heightened rigor in fragile or conflict-affected settings.

### Interaction with Other Performance Standards

PS4 operates within a division of labor across the Performance Standards framework:

- **PS1** — supplies the ESMS architecture, risk identification process, and stakeholder engagement/grievance mechanism through which PS4 requirements are implemented.
- **PS2** — occupational health and safety requirements for workers are included in Performance Standard 2, distinguishing worker-facing OHS from PS4's community-facing scope.
- **PS3** — environmental standards to avoid or minimize impacts on human health and the environment due to pollution are included in Performance Standard 3.
- **PS5** — community safety risks arising from land acquisition/resettlement dynamics interface with PS4's broader safety lens.
- **PS6** — where ecosystem-service-driven community health risks are unavoidable, the client will implement mitigation measures in accordance with paragraphs 24 and 25 of Performance Standard 6.

### Vulnerable Groups: A Cross-Cutting Lens

PS4's attention to vulnerable groups is not confined to a single clause but is applied as a differentiated-risk lens across every domain: infrastructure safety, disease exposure, security interactions, and emergency response planning must all account for populations with heightened susceptibility or diminished capacity to avoid or respond to risk (e.g., children, elderly persons, persons with disabilities, and women in certain sociocultural contexts). Where risks are potentially severe or life-threatening, the client will exercise special care to avoid or minimize their exposure by modifying, substituting, or eliminating the condition or activity causing the risk.

### Reference Instruments and Supplementary Guidance

- **Guidance Note 4** — explains the requirements contained in the Performance Standard, including reference materials, and on good sustainability practices to improve project performance; Guidance Notes are not intended to establish policy by themselves.
- **Good Practice Handbook: Use of Security Forces: Assessing and Managing Risks and Impacts** — the dedicated IFC technical reference for the security personnel management component of PS4.
- **World Bank Group General EHS Guidelines** — technical benchmark for hazardous materials management, traffic safety, and related domains.
- Where mitigation measures require action by third parties, such as national or local governments, the client should, if permitted by the relevant governmental agency, be prepared to work with them in order to find a solution that helps meet the requirements of Performance Standard 4.

### Practical Example

**Example:** A large-scale open-pit mining project employs a private security contractor to protect equipment yards and restrict unauthorized access to blasting zones near an adjacent rural community.

- **Infrastructure/design safety:** Blast zones are set back from community settlements per international quarry-safety standards; warning-siren protocols are established for blast events.
- **Hazardous materials:** Cyanide and other processing reagents are transported and stored under EHS Guideline-aligned containment protocols to prevent community exposure via water or soil pathways.
- **Disease exposure:** An influx of contracted workers into a temporary camp raises risk of communicable disease transmission to the host community; the client implements a workforce health screening and community health outreach program.
- **Security personnel:** The security contract includes a binding code of conduct on community interaction, prohibits arming of personnel beyond what is proportionate and legally justified, and mandates use-of-force training; an independent grievance channel allows community members to report security-related incidents.
- **Emergency preparedness:** The EPRP includes a community-facing tailings-dam-failure warning and evacuation protocol, tested through periodic drills involving local emergency responders.
- **Vulnerable groups:** School siting and children's routes to school are assessed against blast-zone and haul-road traffic risk, given children's differentiated vulnerability to traffic and noise/vibration hazards.

This layered response illustrates how PS4's separate requirement domains function together as a single CHSS risk-management program rather than isolated compliance checkboxes. ### Key Points

- PS4 governs risks a project imposes on surrounding communities (health, safety, security) — distinct from PS2's worker-facing occupational health and safety scope.
- Implementation runs through the PS1 ESMS; PS4 does not create a parallel management system.
- Core domains: infrastructure/equipment design safety, hazardous materials exposure, ecosystem-service-linked health risks, communicable disease exposure, emergency preparedness and response, and security personnel management.
- Security force management is a defining and distinctive element of PS4, addressing both public and private security arrangements, use-of-force proportionality, and community relationship risk.
- Vulnerable groups receive differentiated consideration across every requirement domain, not as a separate standalone clause.
- Conflict-sensitivity ("do no harm") is an explicit concern — projects can exacerbate scarce-resource stress or local tensions even without a direct safety incident.
- PS4 interfaces closely with PS1 (ESMS/EPRP), PS2 (worker OHS boundary), PS3 (pollution-related health impacts), PS5 (resettlement-related safety), and PS6 (ecosystem-service mitigation cross-reference).
