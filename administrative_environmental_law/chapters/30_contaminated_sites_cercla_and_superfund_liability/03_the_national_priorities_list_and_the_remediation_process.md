## The National Priorities List and the Remediation Process


### Overview

The National Priorities List (NPL) is EPA's roster of the most seriously contaminated sites in the United States, prioritized for long-term remedial action under CERCLA. Listing on the NPL triggers eligibility for federally financed cleanup under the Superfund Trust Fund and sets in motion a structured, multi-phase remediation process governed by the National Contingency Plan (NCP). Understanding the NPL mechanism and the remediation sequence that follows is central to CERCLA practice because nearly every major PRP liability dispute, cost-recovery action, or settlement negotiation is anchored to a specific phase of this process.

### Statutory and Regulatory Basis

- **CERCLA §105(a)(8)(B)** — 42 U.S.C. §9605(a)(8)(B): requires EPA to establish criteria for prioritizing sites and to publish the NPL
- **CERCLA §121** — 42 U.S.C. §9621: cleanup standards for remedial actions
- **National Contingency Plan (NCP)** — 40 C.F.R. Part 300: the master regulatory framework implementing CERCLA's response process
- **Hazard Ranking System (HRS)** — 40 C.F.R. Part 300, Appendix A: the scoring methodology for NPL listing

### The National Priorities List

#### **Key Points**

- The NPL is a **list, not itself a cleanup order** — listing identifies a site as a priority for remedial action but does not by itself compel any PRP to act or establish liability.
- EPA is statutorily required to update the NPL **at least annually** (§105(a)(8)(B)).
- Listing determinations are made through **notice-and-comment rulemaking** in the Federal Register and are subject to judicial review exclusively in the **U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit** under CERCLA §113(a).
- As of recent EPA reporting, the NPL contains **over 1,300 sites** nationwide, spanning industrial facilities, mining sites, landfills, and federal facilities. **[Unverified]** — exact current count should be confirmed against EPA's most recent NPL update given periodic additions and deletions.

#### The Hazard Ranking System (HRS)

The HRS is the principal scoring tool used to determine whether a site qualifies for NPL listing:

- Sites are scored across four exposure pathways: **groundwater**, **surface water**, **soil exposure/subsurface intrusion**, and **air**.
- Each pathway is evaluated based on: (1) **likelihood of release**, (2) **waste characteristics** (toxicity and quantity), and (3) **targets** (population and sensitive environments potentially affected).
- A site achieving an HRS score of **28.50 or above** (on a 0–100 scale) generally qualifies for NPL listing, though EPA retains some additional listing mechanisms.

#### Alternative Listing Mechanisms

Beyond the HRS score threshold, a site may be added to the NPL through:

1. **State or territory top priority designation** — each state may designate one top-priority site for listing regardless of HRS score.
2. **Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry (ATSDR) health advisory** — where ATSDR issues a health advisory recommending removal of a population from a site and EPA determines the site poses a significant threat.
3. **Superfund Alternative Approach (SAA)** — not a formal NPL listing mechanism, but an EPA program allowing PRPs to conduct NPL-caliber cleanups under an enforceable agreement without the site being formally listed, often used to avoid stigma associated with NPL designation.

#### Consequences of NPL Listing

- Eligibility for Superfund Trust Fund financing where no viable PRP exists or PRPs are unwilling/unable to fund cleanup.
- Signals to potential purchasers, lenders, and insurers that the property carries significant contamination and liability exposure, which can affect property value and financing (a practical, non-statutory consequence).
- Establishes a presumption of federal government interest and oversight that shapes subsequent enforcement negotiations.
- **Does not**, standing alone, establish PRP liability — liability determinations proceed independently under §107(a).

### The Remediation Process (Overview of NCP-Governed Phases)

The remediation process under the NCP follows a generally sequential structure, though phases can overlap and vary based on emergency circumstances.

#### **1. Preliminary Assessment/Site Inspection (PA/SI)**

- Initial evaluation of a reported or suspected release to determine whether further investigation is warranted.
- Involves review of historical records, aerial photography, and limited sampling.
- Results feed into HRS scoring for potential NPL listing.

#### **2. Removal Actions**

- **Short-term, often emergency** responses addressing immediate threats — distinct from long-term remedial action.
- Can occur at NPL or non-NPL sites, and may proceed **independently of and prior to** any formal NPL listing decision.
- Statutory cost cap generally limits removal actions to **$2 million and 12 months** duration absent an emergency or consistency exemption (subject to periodic adjustment).
- Examples: removing leaking drums, fencing off a site, providing alternative water supplies to an affected community.

#### **3. Remedial Investigation/Feasibility Study (RI/FS)**

- Conducted at NPL sites (or SAA sites) following listing.
- **Remedial Investigation (RI)**: characterizes the nature and extent of contamination — sampling soil, groundwater, sediment, and air; delineating contaminant plumes; assessing risk to human health and the environment through a baseline risk assessment.
- **Feasibility Study (FS)**: develops and evaluates a range of remedial alternatives against the **nine NCP evaluation criteria** (40 C.F.R. §300.430(e)(9)):
  1. Overall protection of human health and the environment
  2. Compliance with Applicable or Relevant and Appropriate Requirements (**ARARs**)
  3. Long-term effectiveness and permanence
  4. Reduction of toxicity, mobility, or volume through treatment
  5. Short-term effectiveness
  6. Implementability
  7. Cost
  8. State acceptance
  9. Community acceptance

#### **4. Record of Decision (ROD)**

- EPA's formal, legally binding document selecting the remedy for the site, based on the RI/FS and public comment.
- Establishes cleanup standards, remedial technology, and performance goals.
- Subject to public comment period and, where the selected remedy departs significantly from the proposed plan, additional justification requirements.
- A ROD can later be modified through an **Explanation of Significant Differences (ESD)** for minor changes or a **ROD Amendment** for fundamental changes.

#### **5. Remedial Design/Remedial Action (RD/RA)**

- **Remedial Design (RD)**: engineering design of the selected remedy (e.g., pump-and-treat system specifications, cap design, treatment train engineering).
- **Remedial Action (RA)**: physical construction and implementation of the remedy.
- Often implemented by PRPs pursuant to a **consent decree** with EPA, or by EPA directly (with subsequent cost recovery against PRPs) where PRPs are unwilling or unable to perform.

#### **6. Operation and Maintenance (O&M)**

- Long-term monitoring and system upkeep following construction completion (e.g., groundwater treatment system operation, cap maintenance, institutional control enforcement).
- Can continue for decades, particularly for groundwater remedies.

#### **7. Five-Year Reviews**

- Required under **CERCLA §121(c)** where a remedy leaves hazardous substances remaining on-site above levels allowing unrestricted use and unlimited exposure.
- EPA reassesses whether the remedy remains protective of human health and the environment.

#### **8. Deletion from the NPL**

- Occurs when EPA determines no further response action is required, typically after remedy completion and confirmation of protectiveness.
- Requires notice-and-comment rulemaking, mirroring the listing process.
- **Partial deletion** is available for discrete, fully remediated portions of a larger site.

---

### Cleanup Standards Under §121

#### **Key Points**

- **Preference for permanent solutions and treatment** — §121(b)(1) establishes a statutory preference for remedies that permanently and significantly reduce the volume, toxicity, or mobility of hazardous substances through treatment, rather than remedies relying primarily on containment or land disposal.
- **ARARs compliance** — remedies must comply with Applicable or Relevant and Appropriate Requirements, meaning cleanup standards must meet the more stringent of federal or state environmental standards applicable to the substance and site conditions (e.g., Safe Drinking Water Act MCLs for groundwater, RCRA land disposal restrictions).
- **State involvement** — states with more stringent standards than federal requirements may have those standards apply as ARARs, provided they are promulgated, identified in a timely manner, and consistently applied.

### **Example**

A former industrial solvent-recycling facility is reported to EPA after nearby residents complain of odors and well-water discoloration. The process might unfold as follows:

1. EPA conducts a **PA/SI**, finding elevated VOC concentrations in soil and adjacent private wells.
2. EPA undertakes an **emergency removal action**, providing bottled water and, later, connecting affected residences to municipal water — completed within the statutory removal cost/time constraints.
3. Based on HRS scoring reflecting the groundwater pathway threat to a populated area, EPA proposes the site for the **NPL**, and after notice-and-comment, finalizes the listing.
4. EPA (or a PRP group under an Administrative Settlement Agreement and Order on Consent, "AOC") conducts the **RI/FS**, delineating a groundwater plume and evaluating remedial alternatives including pump-and-treat, in-situ chemical oxidation, and monitored natural attenuation.
5. EPA issues a **ROD** selecting a combination of source-area soil excavation and a groundwater pump-and-treat system, citing the §121(b) treatment preference and MCL-based ARARs for the affected aquifer.
6. PRPs (identified under §107(a) as past owner/operators and arrangers) enter a **consent decree** to perform **RD/RA**.
7. Following remedy construction, the site enters **O&M**, with **five-year reviews** confirming continued protectiveness as groundwater concentrations decline toward cleanup goals.
8. Upon achieving cleanup levels, EPA proposes **deletion** from the NPL following notice-and-comment.

### Comparative Table: Removal vs. Remedial Actions

| Feature | Removal Action | Remedial Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Short-term, often emergency | Long-term, planned |
| NPL listing required | No | Effectively yes (or SAA equivalent) |
| Statutory cost/time cap | $2 million / 12 months (absent exemption) | No comparable cap |
| Governing process step | Immediate response | RI/FS → ROD → RD/RA |
| Example | Drum removal, temporary water supply | Groundwater pump-and-treat, cap construction |
