## IMDG and CTU Code Training Programs


### Overview

The IMDG (International Maritime Dangerous Goods) Code and the CTU Code (IMO/ILO/UNECE Code of Practice for Packing of Cargo Transport Units) together form the core international training and compliance framework governing the safe packing, handling, and sea transport of dangerous goods and general cargo within cargo transport units (CTUs). For heavy-lift and specialized logistics operators, these two codes intersect directly with load-securing practice, personnel training obligations, and documentation requirements whenever specialized or project cargo involves hazardous materials, or is packed into or onto containers, flat racks, or other CTUs.

---

### IMDG Code: Regulatory Foundation and Training Mandate

**Key Points**

- The IMDG Code mandates a formal training obligation structured around three components: general awareness/familiarization training, function-specific training, and safety training, applicable to any person whose job function involves dangerous goods transport by sea.
- **General awareness/familiarization training**: each person shall be trained in order to be familiar with the general provisions of the IMDG Code, including a description of the nine different classes of dangerous goods; the identification and use of hazard communication methods (labels, package marks, placards, and the shipping document); the use of other documents such as the Container/Vehicle Packing Certificate; requirements for packing, packaging, and packages; segregation and compatibility provisions; and a description of available emergency response documents.
- **Function-specific training**: each person shall be trained in the specific dangerous goods transport provisions applicable to the function that person performs — the Code provides an indicative list, for guidance purposes only, of functions typically found in dangerous goods transport operations by sea and the corresponding training content required for each.
- **Training records**: records of training received shall be kept by the employer and made available to the employee or competent authority upon request, and records must be retained by the employer for a specified period of time as defined in the Code.
- This training shall be provided or verified upon employment in a position involving dangerous goods transport — meaning IMDG training compliance is triggered by role assignment, not merely by voluntary professional development.

---

### IMDG Function-Specific Training Categories

The Code's indicative function list directly reflects the range of roles across a heavy-lift/specialized cargo operation that may require IMDG training, including roles specific to loading and packing cargo transport units.

**Key Points**

- For the "Load/unload cargo transport units" function specifically, required training content includes: documentation, classes, marking/labelling/placarding, stowage requirements (where applicable), segregation requirements, cargo securing requirements (as contained in the CTU Code), emergency response procedures, first aid measures, CSC (Container Safety Convention) requirements, and safe handling procedures — this function explicitly cross-references the CTU Code as the governing standard for cargo securing.
- For the "Prepare transport documents for dangerous goods" function, required content includes documentation requirements such as the transport document, the container/vehicle packing certificate, competent authorities' approval, waste transport documentation, and special documentation where appropriate.
- IMDG training certification is broadly required for dangerous goods employees who perform one or more of the following functions: classification and identification of dangerous goods based on hazard class; packing and/or selecting appropriate packaging; marking and labeling of dangerous goods packages; preparing required shipping paperwork; determining segregation requirements of dangerous goods packages loaded into CTUs; loading and unloading shipments containing dangerous goods; selecting or affixing placards on CTUs; and blocking and bracing dangerous goods within CTUs and/or completing container certification for CTUs.
- IMDG training programs are also needed for freight forwarders, contractors, and other people involved in the process of shipping dangerous goods by ocean — extending the training obligation beyond direct handling personnel to the broader logistics chain supporting a dangerous goods shipment.

---

### Recertification and Currency

**Key Points**

- Employees who have undergone initial or recurrent IMDG training in the past are required by law to undergo recurrent training at least once every three years, based on requirements outlined in 49 CFR (U.S. federal hazardous materials regulations) and the IMDG Code.
- IMDG training coverage typically includes properly classifying or understanding the classification requirements of substances and articles according to their hazard class, assigning or verifying the assignment of the correct identification name and UN number, selecting or verifying correct packaging and understanding applicable packing requirements, and selecting or applying correct marks, labels, and placards for the material being shipped.
- Specialized topics such as lithium battery transport and limited-quantity shipments are commonly offered as distinct add-on training modules alongside baseline Initial and Recurrent training, reflecting how specific cargo categories can carry additional or distinct regulatory requirements within the broader IMDG framework.

---

### The CTU Code: Scope and Purpose

The CTU Code refers to the IMO/ILO/UNECE Code of Practice for Packing of Cargo Transport Units, jointly developed by the International Maritime Organization (IMO), the International Labour Organization (ILO), and the United Nations Economic Commission for Europe (UNECE) to provide guidelines for practice and training in loading goods into transport units.

**Key Points**

- The aim of the CTU Code is to provide persons responsible for packing and loading of transport units (CTUs — e.g., cargo containers or swap bodies) with tips and procedural rules for the safe packing of cargo transport units.
- A cargo transport unit is defined (per the IMDG Code's own cross-reference) as a road transport tank or freight vehicle, a railway transport tank or freight wagon, a multimodal freight container or portable tank, or an MEGC (Multiple-Element Gas Container) — in practical terms, a CTU can be thought of as a large metal box or vehicle used to contain packagings of cargo, including dangerous goods.
- Anyone who loads or unloads cargo transport units (CTUs) must be trained according to section 1.3.1.2 of the IMDG Code in accordance with the CTU Code — meaning CTU Code training is not an optional supplement but a component of the IMDG Code's own mandatory training structure for this specific function.
- The person who packs and secures the cargo into/onto the transport unit is often the last person to visually inspect inside the unit until it is opened at its final destination — a framing used by CTU Code training providers to underscore the outsized safety responsibility resting on packing personnel, since packing errors may go undetected for the remainder of the cargo's journey.
- Poor CTU packing is described by specialized training providers as responsible for an alarmingly high number of incidents leading to damage, loss, injuries, and fatalities — directly motivating the CTU Code's practical, hands-on training emphasis.

### Documentation: The Container/Vehicle Packing Certificate

**Key Points**

- The person(s) responsible for packing dangerous goods in a CTU shall provide a "container/vehicle packing certificate" per section 5.4.2 of the IMDG Code; this document is not required for tanks specifically.
- Before packing, the CTU itself must be checked to ensure it is fit for its intended purpose; the interior and exterior of the CTU must be inspected prior to loading to ensure there is no damage that could affect its integrity or that of the packages it is to contain.
- Condition of packages must also be verified before loading: packages shall not be loaded into a CTU if found to be damaged, leaking, or sifting, and excessive water, snow, ice, or foreign matter adhering to packages must be removed before packing into the CTU.
- Flexible bulk containers are not permitted to be transported within CTUs, per the relevant IMDG Code provision governing bulk container transport.
- Guidelines for use in practice and in training for loading goods into transport units have been drawn up jointly by IMO, ILO, and UNECE and published by IMO as the CTU Code — directly linking the packing certificate documentation requirement back to the CTU Code's training and practice guidance.

---

### Available Training Delivery Formats

**Key Points**

- CTU Code and container-packing training is commonly delivered through both e-learning and on-site/in-person formats: e-learning courses (e.g., a Container Packers Course and Flatrack Cargo Securing Course) provide overviews of key considerations for packing freight containers or securing cargo on flat racks, described by providers as a cost-effective way of keeping up to date with the latest regulations.
- On-site CTU Code seminars are also offered, where training providers travel to the client's site to train employees using their actual products, containers, and loading equipment — a training approach reported by at least one provider to run one full day (8 hours) at the client's site, explicitly aimed at making training as practical and context-relevant as possible.
- On-site CTU Code training is aimed at all persons who handle containers, including shipping personnel, dangerous goods supervisors, shipping company employees, container packing companies, and loading personnel across a wide variety of fields — particularly persons who load containers or are otherwise involved in the loading process.
- IMDG e-learning course content commonly spans an introduction to the Code, general provisions, dangerous goods classification and identification, packing requirements, package marking and labeling, CTU marking and placarding, documentation and completion requirements, stowage requirements, segregation requirements (both within a CTU and for CTUs loaded on vessels), limited/excepted quantities provisions, incident and fire prevention, emergency response procedures in port and onboard ship, and training on the International Convention for Safe Containers (CSC).

---

### Relevance to Heavy-Lift and Specialized Logistics Practice

**Key Points**

- For heavy-lift and project cargo operators, the Flatrack Cargo Securing Course category is of particular practical relevance, since oversized components frequently move on flat rack containers rather than standard enclosed containers, requiring cargo-securing expertise (stability, lashing techniques) distinct from standard box-container packing.
- Personnel involved in blocking and bracing dangerous goods within CTUs, or completing container certification for CTUs, fall within IMDG's function-specific training requirements — directly relevant wherever heavy-lift project cargo includes hazardous components (e.g., certain industrial equipment, chemicals, or batteries) requiring both securing expertise and dangerous-goods documentation competency.
- Given the CSC (International Convention for Safe Containers) requirements referenced within IMDG's function-specific training content, container/CTU structural safety inspection is treated as integrated with, rather than separate from, dangerous goods packing training — relevant to any specialized logistics operation using containerized equipment for oversized or heavy components.
- **Behavior may vary**: [Behavioral disclaimer] IMDG Code editions are updated on a periodic amendment cycle (the Code excerpted here reflects Amendment 42-24, 2024 Edition), and specific provisions, section numbering, and recurrent-training intervals may change between amendment cycles or by jurisdiction (e.g., variations under 49 CFR in the U.S.); training program content and applicable regulatory citations should be verified against the current IMDG Code edition and applicable national regulations before being used for compliance planning.
