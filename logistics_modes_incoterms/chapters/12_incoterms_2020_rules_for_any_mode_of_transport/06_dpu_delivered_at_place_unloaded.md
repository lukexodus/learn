## DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded)

### Overview

DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded) requires the seller to deliver goods by unloading them from the arriving means of transport and making them available to the buyer at the named place of destination. DPU is the only Incoterms rule among all 11 that requires the seller to unload the goods, making it the highest-obligation term within Group D short of DDP's added duty-payment requirement. DPU was introduced in the 2020 edition as the renamed and clarified successor to DAT (Delivered at Terminal) from the 2010 edition.

### Applicable Transport Mode

- DPU belongs to the "Any Mode or Modes of Transport" category, suitable for road, rail, air, sea, or multimodal combinations.

### Evolution from DAT to DPU

- **Key Points**
  - Under the 2010 edition, this rule was named **DAT (Delivered at Terminal)** and was restricted to delivery at a "terminal" — a somewhat ambiguous term that practitioners interpreted variously as a port terminal, container yard, warehouse, or similar facility.
  - The 2020 edition renamed the rule to **DPU (Delivered at Place Unloaded)** and broadened the named location to any place the parties agree upon — not just a terminal — while retaining the core requirement that the seller must unload the goods.
  - The name change and repositioning (DPU now appears after DAP in the sequence, rather than before) reflects the ICC's intent to clarify that "place" is the operative concept, with unloading being the term's distinguishing feature, rather than tying the rule conceptually to a specific facility type.
  - [Inference] This change addressed practical confusion where "terminal" under DAT was sometimes read too narrowly, excluding delivery to a buyer's own warehouse or a non-terminal location even where unloading by the seller was the parties' clear intent.

### Key Points: Obligation Summary

- **Delivery point**: the named place of destination, which may be any location the parties agree upon (a terminal, warehouse, construction site, or other facility).
- **Risk transfer**: occurs only after the goods have been unloaded from the arriving means of transport at the named place — this is the critical distinguishing feature from DAP, where risk transfers before unloading.
- **Unloading**: the seller's responsibility, cost, and risk — the seller must have the equipment, labor, or subcontracted unloading service arranged to complete this at the named destination.
- **Export clearance**: the seller's responsibility.
- **Import clearance**: the buyer's responsibility (distinguishing DPU from DDP).
- **Carriage**: the seller arranges and pays for the entire carriage to the named destination, inclusive of unloading.
- **Insurance**: neither party is obligated to insure under DPU itself; the seller bears risk for the entire journey plus unloading and should insure accordingly.

### Article-by-Article Breakdown (Seller/Buyer)

| Article | Seller Obligation (A) | Buyer Obligation (B) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. General Obligations | Provide goods and invoice per contract | Pay the price |
| 2. Delivery | Unload goods and make available at named place | Take delivery once unloaded |
| 3. Transfer of Risk | Ends only after unloading is complete | Begins after unloading is complete |
| 4. Carriage | Contract and pay for carriage, including unloading, to named place | No obligation to contract carriage |
| 5. Insurance | No obligation, but bears risk through unloading | No obligation |
| 6. Delivery/Transport Document | Provide transport document as needed | Accept conforming document |
| 7. Export/Import Clearance | Handle export clearance | Handle import clearance |
| 8. Checking/Packaging/Marking | Package and mark goods appropriately | No standard obligation |
| 9. Allocation of Costs | All costs to named place, including unloading | Costs from unloading completion onward, plus import duties |
| 10. Notices | Notify buyer of dispatch and expected unloading time | Notify seller of any buyer-controlled requirements |

### Example

A construction equipment manufacturer in Japan sells heavy machinery to a buyer in Indonesia under "DPU Jakarta Construction Site Incoterms® 2020." The seller arranges and pays for ocean freight and inland trucking to the named construction site, and — critically — must also arrange the specialized heavy-lift equipment needed to unload the machinery from the truck at the site. Risk of loss or damage remains with the seller throughout the entire journey and continues through the unloading process itself; only once the machinery is fully unloaded and resting at the site does risk transfer to the buyer. If the machinery is damaged during the unloading operation due to equipment failure, the seller bears that loss, not the buyer.

### DPU vs. DAP: The Critical Distinction

| Feature | DAP | DPU |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unloading responsibility | Buyer | **Seller** |
| Risk transfer point | Ready for unloading, still on vehicle | **After unloading is complete** |
| Seller's exposure to unloading risk | None | Full exposure (damage during unloading is seller's risk) |
| Practical requirement | None beyond arranging carriage | Seller must arrange unloading capability at destination |

- [Inference] Because DPU requires the seller to arrange unloading — which may require specialized equipment unavailable to a foreign seller, or unfamiliarity with local labor/equipment regulations at the destination — DAP is often preferred by sellers who want to avoid the operational complexity and liability exposure that comes with performing or subcontracting unloading in an unfamiliar country.

### Practical Considerations

- **Seller's unloading capability**: sellers considering DPU should confirm they can reliably arrange unloading equipment and labor at the named destination, either directly or through a subcontracted logistics provider, since failure to unload as agreed constitutes a delivery failure under the contract.
- **Named place precision**: as with DAP, the contract should specify the named place with sufficient precision (e.g., a specific loading dock or site address) to avoid disputes over exactly where and how unloading must occur.
- **Liability during unloading**: because risk remains with the seller throughout unloading, sellers should ensure their insurance coverage extends through this operation, not just through transit, since standard transit-only policies may not cover unloading-related damage.

### When DPU Is Typically Used

- Large or heavy cargo (machinery, construction equipment, project cargo) where the seller or its logistics partner has specialized unloading capability the buyer may lack.
- Situations where the buyer explicitly wants a fully "delivered and unloaded" service level, shifting all logistics complexity (except import clearance) to the seller.
- Delivery to sites lacking buyer-side unloading infrastructure (e.g., remote construction sites), where the seller's logistics provider is better equipped to handle unloading.

### Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception**: DPU is simply DAT renamed with no substantive change.

  **Clarification**: While the core unloading requirement is unchanged, the 2020 edition broadened the named location from "terminal" specifically to any agreed place, clarifying applicability beyond port/terminal facilities.
- **Misconception**: DPU and DDP both require the seller to handle import duties.

  **Clarification**: DPU requires the seller only to unload the goods; import clearance and duty payment remain the buyer's responsibility. Only DDP shifts import clearance and duties to the seller.
- **Misconception**: Risk under DPU transfers at the same point as under DAP.

  **Clarification**: DAP transfers risk when goods are ready for unloading (before unloading); DPU transfers risk only after unloading is complete — a materially later point exposing the seller to unloading-related risk that does not exist under DAP.

**Key Points**

- DPU is the only Incoterms rule requiring the seller to unload goods at the named destination; risk transfers only after unloading is complete.
- Renamed from DAT (2010) to DPU (2020), broadening the named location from "terminal" to any agreed place.
- Applicable to any transport mode; import clearance and duties remain the buyer's responsibility (unlike DDP).
- Sellers should confirm unloading capability and insurance coverage through the unloading process before committing to DPU terms.
