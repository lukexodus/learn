## IFC Performance Standard 5 on land acquisition and resettlement

### Overview and Regulatory Context

IFC Performance Standard 5 (PS5), *Land Acquisition and Involuntary Resettlement*, is one of the eight Performance Standards within IFC's 2012 Sustainability Framework. Performance Standard 5 recognises that project-related land acquisition and restrictions on land use can have adverse impacts on communities and persons that use this land, and involuntary resettlement refers both to physical and economic displacement.

The International Finance Corporation Performance Standards, specifically Performance Standard 5 on land acquisition and involuntary resettlement, are widely considered to be the global standard for private sector actors, and it also forms the basis of most mining industry policy commitments on this topic. As with the other topic-specific standards, the implementation of the actions necessary to meet the requirements of this Performance Standard is managed through the client's Environmental and Social Management System, and PS5 is triggered through the risk and impact identification process established under PS1.

### Defining Involuntary Resettlement

Resettlement is considered involuntary when affected persons or communities do not have the right to refuse land acquisition or restrictions on land use that result in physical or economic displacement. This occurs in cases of lawful expropriation or temporary or permanent restrictions on land use, as well as negotiated settlements in which the buyer can resort to expropriation or impose legal restrictions on land use if negotiations with the seller fail.

Involuntary resettlement occurs when affected people do not have the right to refuse land acquisition and are displaced, which may result in long-term hardship and impoverishment as well as social stress. This distinguishes involuntary resettlement from voluntary land transactions, where the seller retains a genuine choice to decline.

**Two forms of displacement are addressed:**

1. **Physical displacement** — relocation, loss of residential land, or loss of shelter.
2. **Economic displacement** — loss of assets or access to assets that leads to loss of income sources or means of livelihood, which can occur even where no physical relocation takes place (i.e., even where no land acquisition or land use restriction results in relocation, purely restricting access to assets or resources can still constitute economic displacement).

### Objectives

PS5 sets out objectives oriented around the avoid-minimize-compensate-restore sequence:

- To avoid, and when avoidance is not possible, minimize displacement by exploring alternative project designs.
- To avoid forced eviction.
- To anticipate and avoid, or where avoidance is not possible, minimize adverse social and economic impacts from land acquisition or restrictions on land use by (i) providing compensation for loss of assets at replacement cost and (ii) ensuring that resettlement activities are implemented with appropriate disclosure of information, consultation, and the informed participation of those affected.
- To improve, or restore, the livelihoods and standards of living of displaced persons.
- To improve living conditions among physically displaced persons through the provision of adequate housing with security of tenure at resettlement sites.

### Compensation Principles

PS5's compensation framework is one of its most technically detailed and consequential components for community engagement and SIA practitioners:

- **Full replacement cost** — clients must provide compensation for loss of assets at full replacement cost and other assistance to help affected persons improve or restore their standards of living or livelihoods.
- **Transparency and consistency** — compensation standards will be transparent and applied consistently to all communities and persons affected by the displacement.
- **Land-based compensation preference** — where livelihoods of displaced persons are land-based, or where land is collectively owned, the client will, where feasible, offer the displaced land-based compensation.
- **Timing/sequencing** — the client will take possession of acquired land and related assets only after compensation has been made available and, where applicable, resettlement sites and moving allowances have been provided to the displaced persons in addition to compensation.
- **Valuation methodology** — the valuation method for determining replacement cost should be documented and included in applicable Resettlement and/or Livelihood Restoration plans.

### Planning Instruments

PS5 requires different planning documents depending on displacement type and scale:

| Instrument | Applies When |
| --- | --- |
| **Resettlement Action Plan (RAP)** | Physical displacement is involved; specifies the procedures that will be followed and the actions that will be taken to mitigate adverse effects, compensate losses, and provide development benefits to persons affected |
| **Livelihood Restoration Plan (LRP)** | Economic displacement without physical relocation (or alongside it) |
| **Abbreviated Resettlement Plan** | Lower-magnitude displacement, scaled to risk |
| **Process Framework** | Restrictions on access to legally designated parks/protected areas without physical relocation |

For projects with significant involuntary resettlement risks, the client will retain competent resettlement professionals to provide advice on compliance with this Performance Standard and to verify the client's monitoring information.

### Community Engagement and Participation

Active community engagement throughout the process is essential, and PS5 requires that, where permitted by the responsible agency, affected persons and communities play an active role in resettlement planning, implementation, and monitoring. Core participatory mechanisms include:

- **Informed consultation and participation (ICP)** — enabling affected households and communities to participate in resettlement planning through informed consultation and participation, and to share in various project benefits and thereby improve their living standards.
- **Disclosure of entitlements** — clients must disclose all relevant information, consult with affected persons, and facilitate their engagement in the decision-making process.
- **Grievance mechanism** — a resettlement-specific grievance channel, consistent with PS1's overarching grievance redress requirement, tailored to compensation disputes and entitlement eligibility questions.
- **Eligibility and entitlement determination** — an often-overlooked feature of the standard relates to the complexity of evaluating entitlements and eligibility amongst the affected population — the due diligence process of understanding which resources and rights people use and how they are assigned based on their system of social organisation requires thorough knowledge of land tenure regimes.

### The Impoverishment Risks and Reconstruction (IRR) Model

Guidance Note 5 grounds PS5's socio-economic risk framework in Cernea's widely used **Impoverishment Risks and Reconstruction (IRR) Model** (Cernea, 1997, 2000). The main socio-economic risks associated with involuntary resettlement — and therefore those which need to be addressed by developers — include landlessness, joblessness, homelessness, marginalization, food insecurity, increased morbidity, loss of access to common property resources, and social disarticulation (with PS5 paragraph cross-references provided for each risk category).

### Land Tenure and Non-Titled Users

PS5's protection extends beyond formal titleholders. Compensation eligibility must account for both titled and non-titled land users — including customary rights holders, informal occupants, and those with usufruct or access rights — since a rigid title-only approach would exclude many of the people whose livelihoods are actually affected. Developing a thorough knowledge of land tenure regimes is essential to properly identifying who is entitled to compensation and on what basis.

### Scope Boundary: What PS5 Does Not Cover

PS5's scope has a defined boundary. In the event of impacts by project activities other than land acquisition or restriction of access to land use, the client's social and environmental risks and impacts identification process under Performance Standard 1 should address how these risks and impacts will be avoided, minimized, mitigated, or compensated for by the client. Examples include the loss of access to state-owned sub-surface mineral rights by artisanal miners, or pollution/project-related disruption of access to water on land not acquired by the project or whose use is not restricted by the project — while PS5 will not apply to these situations, the client should nonetheless consider appropriate mitigation through PS1's broader risk-management process.

### Implementation Challenges and Critical Perspectives

PS5 has faced sustained scholarly scrutiny regarding real-world performance. PS5 is in its second decade as the industry standard for guiding involuntary land acquisition and resettlement, and during this time, displacement scholars have built a substantial body of case documentation regarding project performance on displaced persons' rights. Additional complexity is introduced in conflict-affected or fragile contexts: circumstances will add considerable complexity to the usual challenges in land acquisition and involuntary resettlement, and may potentially exacerbate existing land conflict — reinforcing the conflict-sensitivity lens also embedded in PS4.

### Interaction with Other Performance Standards

- **PS1** — supplies the ESMS, risk identification process, and stakeholder engagement/grievance architecture through which PS5 is implemented.
- **PS4** — resettlement processes can generate community health, safety, and security risks (e.g., relocation-related stress, disputes over compensation) that interface with PS4's community safety lens.
- **PS7** — where displaced persons are Indigenous Peoples, PS7's heightened consent and cultural-continuity requirements apply in addition to PS5.
- **PS8** — physical or economic displacement affecting sites of cultural heritage significance triggers PS8 alongside PS5.

### Practical Example

**Example:** A linear infrastructure project (a transmission line corridor) requires a permanent right-of-way easement through agricultural land farmed by 60 households, several of whom hold only customary use rights rather than formal title.

- **Screening:** PS1's risk identification process flags the corridor as triggering PS5 due to anticipated economic displacement (loss of cropland use) with no physical relocation.
- **Entitlement mapping:** A land tenure and asset survey documents both titled landowners and customary users, establishing an entitlement matrix so that non-titled farmers are not excluded from compensation.
- **Compensation approach:** Because affected livelihoods are land-based, the client explores land-based compensation (offering alternate parcels) where feasible, alongside cash compensation at full replacement cost for lost crop cycles during construction.
- **Livelihood Restoration Plan:** For households experiencing partial land loss that reduces farm viability below subsistence thresholds, a livelihood restoration package includes agricultural extension support and access to alternative income-generating training.
- **Consultation and disclosure:** Compensation rates and eligibility criteria are disclosed transparently in community meetings before finalization, and a dedicated grievance mechanism handles disputes over valuation or eligibility.
- **Timing:** Construction access to any given parcel is withheld until compensation has been delivered to the affected household, consistent with the "compensation before possession" sequencing principle.
- **Monitoring:** Post-resettlement livelihood monitoring tracks household income recovery against pre-project baseline for a defined multi-year period, verified by an independent resettlement professional given the project's significant resettlement risk profile.

### Key Points

- PS5 governs both physical displacement (relocation/loss of shelter) and economic displacement (loss of livelihood-generating assets or access), and economic displacement can occur without any relocation.
- The core sequence is avoid → minimize → compensate at full replacement cost → restore/improve livelihoods, with land-based compensation preferred where livelihoods are land-based.
- Compensation must occur before land possession, and resettlement sites/moving allowances must be provided where applicable, in addition to compensation.
- Both titled and non-titled/customary land users must be considered in entitlement determination — a central and complex due diligence task.
- Informed consultation and participation (ICP) is mandatory, with affected persons playing an active role in resettlement planning, implementation, and monitoring where permitted.
- The Cernea IRR Model (landlessness, joblessness, homelessness, marginalization, food insecurity, morbidity, loss of common property access, social disarticulation) frames the socio-economic risks PS5 requires developers to address.
- PS5 has a defined scope boundary — impacts unrelated to land acquisition or access restriction fall under PS1's general risk-management process rather than PS5 itself.
- Real-world implementation has drawn sustained scholarly critique regarding gaps between standard and outcome; practitioners should treat this as an active area of debate.
