## What Values Are, Operationally


In philosophical discourse, values are normative commitments — beliefs about what matters, what is right, what kind of person one ought to be. For operational purposes, a more functional definition is useful:

**A value is a stable decision-making criterion that persists across contexts and resists situational pressure.**

This definition has several implications:

- Values are only demonstrably real when they cost something. A commitment that is never tested is not a verified value — it is a preference.
- Values that exist only as abstract beliefs without behavioral expression are not operational. They will not function under pressure.
- The stability of a value is empirically testable: does your behavior in high-pressure situations track your stated commitments?

**Key Points**

- [Inference] Most people overestimate the stability of their values under novel high-pressure conditions. This is not a moral failing — it reflects that values are rarely stress-tested in ordinary life, so their operational robustness is genuinely unknown until tested.
- The intelligence and military contexts that model this syllabus involve systematic exposure to conditions specifically designed — by adversaries or by operational reality — to erode value stability. Awareness of this is itself a partial countermeasure.
