## Framing the Problem


Moral stress is distinct from performance stress or fear. It arises when action is required in conditions where:

- Values conflict with each other
- The right course of action is genuinely unclear
- External pressure demands behavior inconsistent with internal standards
- Consequences of choices fall on others, not only on yourself
- Retrospective judgment — your own or others' — is anticipated

The problem is not simply emotional discomfort. Moral stress produces specific cognitive and behavioral failure modes: rationalization, value drift, dissociation from consequences, and post-hoc justification of decisions that would not have passed prior scrutiny. These are not character failures in the ordinary sense. They are predictable outputs of identifiable psychological mechanisms operating under pressure.

The goal of this module is not to tell you what your values should be. It is to equip you with the frameworks, mechanisms, and practices that allow whatever values you hold to remain functional — accessible and action-guiding — when conditions are most hostile to them.
