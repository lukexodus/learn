## Cognitive Baseline Assessment


A cognitive baseline serves as the diagnostic foundation of the entire syllabus. Without an accurate self-map of your current capacities, training effort is allocated blindly. This module establishes what to measure, how to measure it, and how to interpret results across three domains: memory, pattern recognition, and linguistic aptitude.

---

### Memory

#### Taxonomy of Memory Systems

Understanding which memory system you are assessing prevents conflation of distinct capacities.

|System|Function|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Sensory (Iconic/Echoic)**|Ultra-short retention of raw perceptual input|Holding a license plate in mind for 2 seconds|
|**Working Memory**|Active manipulation of a small information set|Mental arithmetic; holding context while reading|
|**Episodic Memory**|Autobiographical events with temporal context|Recalling a specific meeting, conversation, or location|
|**Semantic Memory**|General factual and conceptual knowledge|Knowing what a dead drop is|
|**Procedural Memory**|Motor and habitual skill execution|Driving; lock-picking after practice|
|**Prospective Memory**|Remembering to perform a future action|Remembering to check a signal site at a specific time|

For intelligence-relevant work, the highest-priority systems are **working memory**, **episodic memory**, and **prospective memory**.

---

#### Memory Baseline Tests

These are structured self-administered tasks. Record raw scores; do not adjust for perceived effort.

---

##### Working Memory — Digit Span

**Forward span:** Read a random digit string at 1 digit/second (use a random number generator). Immediately reproduce it. Increase length by one digit each trial. Record the longest string reproduced correctly on the first attempt.

- Below 5: Low
- 5–7: Average
- 7–9: High
- 9+: Exceptional

**Backward span:** Same procedure; reproduce digits in reverse order. Backward span is typically 1–2 digits shorter than forward span. A gap larger than 3 indicates working memory inefficiency under manipulation load.

**N-Back (self-administered approximation):** Use a free n-back application (e.g., Brain Workshop, or any validated free tool). Complete 2-back and 3-back sequences. Record accuracy percentage, not subjective difficulty.

|N-Back Level|Accuracy Threshold (functional)|
|---|---|
|2-back|≥ 80%|
|3-back|≥ 65%|

---

##### Episodic Memory — Delayed Recall

**Procedure:** Read a 300–400 word factual passage (news article, encyclopedia entry). After a 30-minute distractor interval (do something unrelated), write down everything you can recall without looking back. Score: count discrete factual propositions recalled correctly.

- Below 10 propositions: Low
- 10–18: Average
- 18–25: High
- 25+: Exceptional

Repeat this test on a second passage 24 hours later without re-reading the first. Decay rate between immediate and delayed recall is a secondary metric. A decay greater than 50% indicates consolidation weakness.

---

##### Prospective Memory

No standardized self-test exists with high reliability.

[Inference] A functional proxy: for one week, set 5 intention-action pairs per day without external reminders (e.g., "at 14:00 I will do X"; no phone alert). Track success rate. This is [Unverified] as a validated psychometric instrument but reflects real-world prospective memory demand.

- Below 60% success: Weak
- 60–80%: Functional
- 80%+: Strong

---

##### Associative Memory — Person-Place-Object

This is directly operationally relevant (e.g., memorizing contact descriptions, locations, cover details).

**Procedure:** Study 15 person-object-location triplets for 5 minutes (e.g., "Tall man, brown coat, left his umbrella at the café on Jaffa Street"). After a 20-minute interval, reproduce as many complete triplets as possible.

- Below 5 complete triplets: Low
- 5–9: Average
- 10–13: High
- 14–15: Exceptional

---

#### What the Memory Baseline Reveals

A strong forward digit span with a weak backward span points to passive encoding strength but manipulation weakness — relevant because field work requires not just retaining information but actively reorganizing it under stress.

Weak delayed recall with strong immediate recall isolates a consolidation problem, addressable through spaced repetition. Weak immediate recall more often indicates attentional or encoding-stage failure.

### Pattern Recognition

#### Scope of the Domain

Pattern recognition is not a single faculty. For this syllabus, four sub-types are relevant:

1. **Visual-spatial pattern recognition** — identifying structure, anomaly, or recurrence in physical environments or images
2. **Behavioral pattern recognition** — reading deviation from baseline behavior in individuals or groups
3. **Numerical/logical pattern recognition** — identifying structural regularities in data sequences
4. **Linguistic pattern recognition** — detecting stylistic, syntactic, or semantic regularities in text or speech

---

#### Baseline Tests

##### Visual-Spatial

**Method 1 — Matrix Reasoning (Raven's Progressive Matrices):** The standard IQ-adjacent test for abstract visual pattern completion. Free versions exist online (e.g., 123test.com, though note: online versions are [Unverified] in terms of normative calibration compared to the original Raven's Standard Progressive Matrices).

Use only as a relative self-reference, not as a definitive IQ-equivalent score.

**Method 2 — Kim's Game:** A classical intelligence training exercise attributed to Rudyard Kipling's _Kim_ and reportedly used in various military and intelligence training contexts [Unverified as standard doctrine — treat as widely cited tradecraft lore].

Place 15–20 objects on a table. Study for 60 seconds. Cover. List all objects from memory. This conflates visual attention and short-term memory with pattern encoding. Relevant baseline, but note it is a composite measure.

**Method 3 — Change Blindness Test:** Use an online change blindness paradigm (alternating-image format). Track how quickly you detect changes. This measures attentional allocation to spatial patterns, not memory.

---

##### Behavioral Pattern Recognition

No validated self-test exists for this in isolation.

[Inference] A usable proxy: select 5–10 minutes of footage from a surveillance or documentary source with a crowd or public space. Without audio initially, attempt to identify:

- Who appears anomalous relative to the group baseline
- Who is aware of their environment vs. inward-focused
- Any repeated contact between individuals

Compare your notes to a second viewing with audio. Track your hit rate and false positive rate.

This is [Unverified] as a psychometric instrument but is functionally calibrating for the actual operational skill.

---

##### Numerical/Logical

Use the following sequence types as self-tests. Give yourself 10 minutes, no calculator:

1. Number series completion (e.g., 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, __)
2. Matrix logic puzzles (available in any psychometric prep book — Watson Glaser or similar)
3. Syllogism validity testing (given premises, is conclusion valid — regardless of real-world truth?)

These are well-established cognitive assessment formats. Track raw accuracy and whether errors cluster in a specific type (arithmetic error vs. structural misread vs. missed rule).

---

##### Linguistic Pattern Recognition

This overlaps with linguistic aptitude (see below) but here the focus is on structural detection, not production.

**Authorship attribution exercise:** Take 3 short texts from different unknown authors. Identify which two share the same author based only on style, syntax, lexical density, and sentence rhythm. [Inference] This exercises the same cognitive capacity used in detecting rehearsed cover stories, inconsistency in reported speech, and deception indicators.

**Cipher pattern detection:** Attempt simple substitution cipher resolution without a key. This tests tolerance for ambiguity, hypothesis generation, and rule extraction — all relevant cognitive habits.

### Linguistic Aptitude

#### Why This Is a Distinct Domain

Linguistic aptitude is not the same as language knowledge. It refers to the underlying cognitive capacity to acquire, process, and operate in languages — including under conditions of incomplete information, emotional load, or deception. In intelligence contexts this matters because: language acquisition speed, accent attenuation, code-switching fluency, and the ability to detect non-native speech patterns or scripted affect are all operationally relevant.

---

#### Components of Linguistic Aptitude

The most validated model comes from the **Modern Language Aptitude Test (MLAT)**, developed by Carroll and Sapon. Its four components are:

|Component|Description|
|---|---|
|**Phonetic coding ability**|Encoding and retaining novel sound patterns|
|**Grammatical sensitivity**|Recognizing the grammatical function of words in sentences|
|**Rote learning ability**|Memorizing arbitrary form-meaning pairings rapidly|
|**Inductive language learning**|Inferring rules from samples without explicit instruction|

Each of these maps onto intelligence-specific tasks:

- Phonetic coding → accent acquisition, voice recognition, dialect detection
- Grammatical sensitivity → parsing ambiguous or translated speech for intended meaning vs. literal meaning
- Rote learning → vocabulary acquisition, code names, alias-documentation consistency
- Inductive learning → learning a target's idiolect; detecting scripted vs. spontaneous speech

---

#### Baseline Tests

##### Phonetic Coding

**Method:** Use the LLAMA_B sub-test (from the LLAMA aptitude battery, available free online from Meara/LLAMA research tools). It presents novel sound-symbol pairings and tests retention.

Alternatively: spend 5 minutes learning 20 words from a phonologically unfamiliar language (e.g., Georgian, Swahili, or Welsh if you have no prior exposure). Test yourself 15 minutes later. Recall above 14/20 indicates strong phonetic coding.

---

##### Grammatical Sensitivity

**Method:** Take a paragraph and identify the grammatical role (subject, object, modifier, etc.) of every word without using part-of-speech labels you memorized — reason it through from function. Then take a sentence with deliberate structural ambiguity (e.g., "The man saw the woman with the telescope") and enumerate all valid parse trees.

[Inference] Facility here correlates with the ability to detect when someone is reciting a prepared statement rather than generating speech naturally, since rehearsed language tends to have lower syntactic variation. _Disclaimer: this correlation is not guaranteed; LLM and human behavior under these conditions is not deterministically predictable._

---

##### Rote Learning Speed

**Method:** Create 30 novel word pairs (invent non-words as needed, e.g., "veltok = bridge", "siramu = wait"). Study for 8 minutes. Test without reference. Score out of 30.

- Below 15: Low rote learning speed
- 15–22: Average
- 22–27: High
- 28–30: Exceptional

---

##### Inductive Language Learning

**Method:** Use the LLAMA_D sub-test (available via same LLAMA battery). It presents a text in an unknown language alongside a picture and asks you to infer word meanings. This is the most direct test of this capacity.

Alternative: take a simple grammar pattern in a language you do not know (e.g., a 10-sentence set in Turkish or Finnish with translations) and attempt to construct a new sentence using only inferred rules. Evaluate against a verified translation.

---

#### Idiolect and Register Awareness

Beyond formal aptitude, baseline your **register-switching** and **idiolect sensitivity**:

**Register test:** Write the same informational content (e.g., explaining a route) in three registers: formal written, spoken casual to a peer, spoken casual to an authority. Have someone assess whether the differences are authentic or strained.

**Idiolect sensitivity:** Read 5 short text samples from different individuals on the same topic. Without metadata, cluster them by author. Track your confidence and accuracy.

---

### Integrating the Baseline

After running these tests, map your results across three axes:

```
Memory:              [Weak ——————————— Strong]
Pattern Recognition: [Weak ——————————— Strong]
Linguistic Aptitude: [Weak ——————————— Strong]
```

The relevant question is not your absolute score but your **profile shape**:

|Profile|Implication|
|---|---|
|Strong memory, weak pattern recognition|Good retention, poor anomaly detection — prioritize Phase 1 pattern training|
|Strong pattern recognition, weak rote linguistic learning|Good field reads, poor language acquisition — allocate more time to Phase 2|
|Weak working memory, strong inductive learning|Struggles under load; compensates with structural reasoning — train working memory actively before Phase 3|
|Uniform weakness across all three|No single compensatory strength; build foundations before advancing phases|
|Uniform strength|Proceed to Phase 1 training directly; use this baseline for recalibration mid-course|

---

**Key Points**

- These tests measure current state, not fixed capacity. All three domains are trainable, but rate of improvement varies by individual and is [Unverified] to follow a universal trajectory.
- Do not average scores across domains. A profile of uneven strengths is more actionable than a single composite number.
- Re-administer the same battery after 8 weeks of Phase 1 training. The delta, not the absolute score, is the more reliable signal of whether interventions are working.
- All normative benchmarks above are [Inference] adapted from published cognitive psychology ranges; they have not been validated for this specific application context.
