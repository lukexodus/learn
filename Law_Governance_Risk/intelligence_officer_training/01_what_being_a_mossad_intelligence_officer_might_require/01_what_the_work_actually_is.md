## What the Work Actually Is


Most espionage is not action-movie fieldwork. The core job is **influence over information** — collecting it, protecting it, denying it to adversaries, and turning people who have it into sources.

A Mossad officer's work clusters into a few roles:

**Case Officer (Katsa)** — The primary field role confirmed in memoirs like Victor Ostrovsky's _By Way of Deception_ (disputed but widely cited) and Gad Shimron's accounts. A katsa runs human sources (agents), builds cover identities, and operates in foreign countries without diplomatic protection. This is the rarest and most demanding role.

**Intelligence Analyst** — Processes, interprets, and synthesizes collected information. Less romantic, arguably more consequential.

**Support and Logistics** — Sayanim (diaspora helpers), document forgers, safe house managers, technical operators. Confirmed as a structural feature by multiple investigative journalists including Ronen Bergman (_Rise and Kill First_).

**Technical/Signals** — Cyber and signals intelligence. Unit 8200 (military, not Mossad proper) is well-documented here; Mossad has analogous technical divisions. [Inference: the line between these organizations is deliberately blurred.]
