## Core Deal Vocabulary and Terminology


### Overview

Tax equity transactions rely on a specialized vocabulary drawn from tax law, partnership accounting, and project finance. Fluency with these terms is a prerequisite for reading term sheets, partnership agreements, and tax opinions in this market.

### Foundational Structural Terms

**Partnership Flip**

The dominant tax equity structure in which a developer/sponsor and an investor form a partnership (typically an LLC taxed as a partnership) that owns the project. Allocations of income, loss, and tax credits are heavily weighted toward the investor until it reaches a negotiated target return, at which point allocations "flip" — usually to a small residual investor share and a large majority sponsor share.

**Flip Point / Flip Date**

The point at which the tax equity investor has achieved its target after-tax yield and allocation percentages shift from investor-favored to sponsor-favored. Can be:

- **Fixed flip** — a predetermined calendar date.
- **Yield-based flip** — occurs whenever the investor's target IRR is actually achieved, which may be earlier or later than originally projected depending on performance.

**Target Yield / Target IRR**

The after-tax internal rate of return the tax equity investor is underwriting to receive from the combination of tax credits, depreciation tax shield, and cash distributions.

$$\text{IRR: } \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + \text{IRR})^t} = 0$$

where $CF_t$ includes the initial capital outlay (negative) and subsequent after-tax cash and tax benefit flows (positive).

**Sale-Leaseback**

A structure in which the developer sells the completed project to an investor/lessor (who then claims the ITC and depreciation) and simultaneously leases the asset back to operate it, typically making rent payments that provide the investor's return.

**Inverted Lease (Lease Pass-Through)**

A structure in which the developer retains ownership of the project but elects, under IRC §50(d), to pass through the ITC to a lessee (the tax equity investor), while the developer itself retains the depreciation deductions — effectively the reverse allocation pattern of a sale-leaseback.

### Tax Attribute Terms

**Investment Tax Credit (ITC)**

A one-time credit under IRC §48 (legacy) or §48E (technology-neutral, post-2024) calculated as a percentage of the eligible basis of qualifying property, claimed in the year the property is placed in service.

**Production Tax Credit (PTC)**

A per-kilowatt-hour credit under IRC §45 (legacy) or §45Y (technology-neutral) earned over a 10-year period based on actual electricity generation from a qualifying facility.

**Placed in Service (PIS)**

The date on which a project is considered ready and available for its specifically assigned function, triggering ITC eligibility and the start of depreciation and PTC accrual. A precisely defined and heavily scrutinized term for tax purposes.

**Eligible Basis / Depreciable Basis**

The cost basis of qualifying property used to calculate the ITC and depreciation deductions, generally equal to the property's cost, subject to certain adjustments.

**Basis Reduction**

Under IRC §50(c), when the ITC is claimed, the depreciable basis of the property must be reduced by 50% of the ITC amount claimed, since a full ITC plus full undiminished depreciation would otherwise double-count part of the subsidy.

$$\text{Adjusted Depreciable Basis} = \text{Eligible Basis} - (0.5 \times \text{ITC Claimed})$$

**Recapture**

The clawback of previously claimed ITC (in whole or in part) if the property is disposed of, ceases to qualify, or certain other disqualifying events occur within a defined recapture period (generally 5 years, on a declining schedule), under IRC §50(a).

**Depreciation Recapture**

A related but distinct concept referring to the taxation of previously deducted depreciation as ordinary income upon disposition of the asset at a gain, under IRC §1245/§1250 principles as applied to energy property.

### Capital and Ownership Terms

**Capital Account**

Each partner's economic interest in the partnership, tracked under IRC §704(b) principles, reflecting contributions, allocated income/loss, and distributions. Capital account maintenance rules govern whether special tax allocations (like a disproportionate share of credits to the investor) will be respected by the IRS.

**Substantial Economic Effect**

The standard under Treasury Regulations §1.704-1(b) that special partnership allocations (e.g., 99% of tax benefits to the investor) must meet to be respected for tax purposes, generally requiring that allocations correspond to the actual economic burden or benefit borne by each partner.

**Minimum Gain Chargeback**

A partnership tax mechanism ensuring that if nonrecourse debt is paid down or the property is disposed of, resulting "minimum gain" is allocated in a manner consistent with how related deductions were previously allocated, preserving the integrity of the partnership's allocation scheme.

**Capital Account Deficit Restoration Obligation (DRO)**

A partner's contractual obligation to restore a negative capital account balance upon liquidation, sometimes used (in limited amounts) in tax equity structures to support the economic substance of loss allocations.

### Investor Protection Terms

**Indemnification / Indemnity**

Contractual provisions requiring the sponsor to compensate the investor for specified losses, most critically tax-related losses such as ITC recapture caused by sponsor actions or credit disqualification due to a compliance failure.

**True-Up**

A mechanism adjusting final allocations or payments between partners if actual performance (generation, tax attribute value, or timing) differs from initial projections, ensuring the investor's economic deal is preserved as underwritten.

**Buyout Option / Purchase Option**

A right, typically held by the sponsor, to purchase the investor's remaining interest after the flip point at fair market value, allowing the sponsor to consolidate full ownership.

**Right of First Refusal (ROFR)**

A contractual right, commonly held by the sponsor, giving it the first opportunity to match a third-party offer if the investor seeks to sell its interest.

**Guaranteed Tax Credit Adjustment / Make-Whole Payment**

A payment obligation triggered if the actual tax credit realized differs from what was projected due to a covered event, intended to preserve the investor's target economics.

### Post-IRA Terminology

**Transferability (Transfer Sale)**

Under IRC §6418, the sale of an eligible tax credit for cash to an unrelated taxpayer, without transferring ownership of the underlying project or its depreciation.

**Direct Pay (Elective Payment)**

Under IRC §6417, an election available to tax-exempt entities, governmental bodies, and certain other applicable entities to receive a cash payment from the IRS equal to the value of an otherwise-earned credit, in lieu of using it against tax liability.

**Adders / Bonus Credits**

Additional percentage points added to the base ITC/PTC rate for meeting specified criteria — domestic content, energy community location, or low-income community allocation — introduced or expanded under the IRA.

**Prevailing Wage and Apprenticeship (PWA) Requirements**

Labor standards that, if unmet on qualifying projects above a certain size, reduce the credit rate to a base level (often one-fifth of the full rate); compliance is required to access the full 5x multiplier credit value.
