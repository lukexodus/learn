## Tracking Treasury and IRS Guidance in Real Time


### Overview

Tax equity and tax credit transactions are governed by a layered hierarchy of authority — statute, Treasury regulations, and sub-regulatory IRS guidance (notices, revenue procedures, revenue rulings, private letter rulings) — that evolves continuously and, particularly in the current environment, rapidly. Since the enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) in July 2025, Treasury and the IRS have been issuing a steady stream of interim guidance addressing beginning-of-construction rules, foreign entity of concern (FEOC) restrictions, and safe harbor mechanics. Practitioners in this field must build systematic guidance-tracking practices into their daily workflow, since deal structuring, pricing, and risk allocation decisions depend directly on guidance that can shift materially between term sheet and closing.

### Key Points

- Sub-regulatory guidance (notices, revenue procedures) frequently precedes formal proposed and final regulations by months or years, and deals are often structured based on reliance on interim notices rather than final rules
- Post-OBBBA guidance has been arriving in a compressed timeframe covering FEOC/material assistance rules, beginning-of-construction safe harbors, and domestic content requirements — all directly affecting deal structuring and pricing
- Guidance tracking is not passive reading; it requires active monitoring, internal distribution protocols, and rapid reassessment of deal terms and financial models when new guidance issues
- Reliance periods embedded in interim guidance (e.g., "taxpayers may rely on this notice until 60 days after publication of forthcoming regulations") are themselves critical dates that must be tracked

### The Guidance Hierarchy

Understanding where a given piece of guidance sits in the hierarchy of authority is essential to assessing its reliability and durability:

1. **Statute** (Internal Revenue Code, as amended by legislation such as OBBBA) — highest authority
2. **Treasury Regulations** (final and temporary) — legally binding, but often take years to finalize
3. **Proposed Regulations** — provide insight into Treasury's intended direction but are not binding; taxpayers may sometimes rely on them before finalization
4. **Notices** — sub-regulatory guidance, often issued to provide interim rules while formal regulations are drafted; frequently the primary operative guidance for years in fast-moving policy areas
5. **Revenue Procedures** — administrative procedures, including safe harbor mechanics (e.g., beginning-of-construction safe harbor tables)
6. **Revenue Rulings** — IRS positions on specific factual scenarios
7. **Private Letter Rulings (PLRs)** — binding only on the requesting taxpayer, but informative as to IRS thinking
8. **FAQs and informal guidance** — lowest reliability, generally not citable as authority but useful for directional signal

### Current Guidance Environment (as of September 2026)

Recent developments illustrate why real-time tracking is essential to active deal execution. As of February 2026, Treasury and the IRS issued a notice providing guidance for determining whether electricity-producing qualified facilities, energy storage technologies, or eligible components are receiving material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity and would be ineligible for certain energy tax credits, formally designated Notice 2026-15. This notice addresses restrictions that the One, Big, Beautiful Bill added new restrictions to clean electricity credits under Internal Revenue Code Sections 45Y and 48E, and the advanced manufacturing production credit under Section 45X. [Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-provide-guidance-for-certain-energy-tax-credits-regarding-material-assistance-provided-by-prohibited-foreign-entities-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)[Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-provide-guidance-for-certain-energy-tax-credits-regarding-material-assistance-provided-by-prohibited-foreign-entities-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)

Key mechanics from this notice illustrate the kind of interim-reliance structure practitioners must track:

- Treasury and IRS intend to propose regulations with respect to the definition of a PFE and the calculation of the material assistance cost ratio that taxpayers must use to determine whether there was material assistance from a PFE, meaning the notice is explicitly a placeholder pending formal rulemaking. [Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-provide-guidance-for-certain-energy-tax-credits-regarding-material-assistance-provided-by-prohibited-foreign-entities-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
- Taxpayers may rely on the Notice until 60 days after forthcoming proposed regulations and guidance are published — a reliance window that itself must be monitored, since the clock resets upon publication of the eventual proposed regulations. [Bracewell LLP](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/treasury-and-irs-issue-guidance-on-foreign-entity-of-concern-rules-for-clean-energy-tax-credits/)
- Treasury and the IRS have requested that any comments to the Notice be submitted by March 30, 2026, illustrating the active comment-and-revise cycle typical of major sub-regulatory guidance. [Bracewell LLP](https://www.bracewell.com/resources/treasury-and-irs-issue-guidance-on-foreign-entity-of-concern-rules-for-clean-energy-tax-credits/)
- Notably, the Notice describes beginning construction on or before December 31, 2025 as an example of "anti-circumvention" rather than standard grandfathering and may signal increased IRS scrutiny of facilities beginning construction in 2025 — a subtle interpretive signal with direct implications for safe-harbor documentation practices that a practitioner would only catch through close reading of the notice text, not just headline summaries. [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/february/treasury-and-irs-provide-guidance-regarding-energy-tax-credit-limits)

Separately, earlier domestic content guidance shows the layered, iterative nature of this guidance stream: on January 16, 2025, the Treasury Department and IRS released Notice 2025-08, which provides an updated elective safe harbor for the Domestic Content Requirement that modifies the safe harbor provided in Notice 2024-41 — demonstrating that safe harbor tables themselves are periodically revised and superseded, requiring practitioners to confirm they are working from the current version before relying on any percentage threshold in a deal model. [Tax Notes](https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/irs-guidance/notices/irs-issues-energy-credit-guidance-material-assistance-rules/7tz6z)

**[Inference]** Given that this notice was described by outside counsel as long-awaited guidance, deals that closed in 2025 and early 2026 involving FEOC-sensitive supply chains were likely structured under significant uncertainty, with FEOC compliance representations and indemnities drafted more conservatively pending this clarification. ### Practical Tracking Sources and Methods

**1. Primary government sources**

- **IRS.gov Newsroom** — issues press releases (IR-YYYY-##) alongside formal notices, providing plain-language summaries
- **Federal Register** — publication of proposed and final Treasury regulations, including comment period deadlines
- **Treasury.gov** — Treasury-level policy announcements, often preceding IRS technical guidance
- **IRS Internal Revenue Bulletin (IRB)** — weekly compilation of all official IRS guidance (notices, revenue rulings, revenue procedures)

**2. Professional and industry monitoring services**

- **Tax Notes** and similar specialty tax publications — provide same-day technical analysis of newly issued guidance
- **Law firm client alerts** — major project finance and tax practice groups (e.g., Baker Botts, Bracewell, McGuireWoods, Norton Rose Fulbright, and similar firms with dedicated energy tax practices) typically issue same-day or next-day client alerts analyzing new guidance, often more digestible than reading full notice text first-pass
- **Industry associations** — renewable energy and tax equity-focused industry groups often coordinate joint comment letters and host briefing calls following major guidance releases

**3. Internal firm/organization practices**

- **Guidance distribution protocols**: designated team members responsible for daily/weekly monitoring and internal alert distribution when guidance affecting active deals is released
- **Deal-specific guidance logs**: maintaining a log of which guidance a given deal's tax opinion or model relies upon, so subsequent guidance changes can be quickly cross-referenced against the active pipeline
- **Reliance period tracking**: a calendar or tracker specifically for reliance windows and trigger dates (e.g., "taxpayers may rely on interim safe harbor until 60 days after publication of forthcoming safe harbor tables")

### Why Real-Time Tracking Matters for Deal Execution

- **Tax opinions reference specific guidance**: a tax opinion drafted relying on an interim notice must be revisited if that notice is superseded before closing
- **Financial models embed safe harbor assumptions**: domestic content percentages, FEOC material assistance cost ratios, and beginning-of-construction qualification all flow directly into ITC/PTC value calculations in the model
- **Indemnity and representation drafting**: LLC Agreement representations regarding FEOC compliance, material assistance cost ratios, and domestic content status must reference the guidance version in effect at signing, with mechanisms to address subsequent guidance changes
- **Comment letter participation**: sponsors and investors with material exposure to a guidance area may participate in the comment process (e.g., comments to the Notice be submitted by March 30, 2026) both to influence final rules and to signal industry positions to internal stakeholders

### Example: Practical Impact on an Active Transaction

A sponsor negotiating a partnership flip for a solar-plus-storage project with equipment sourced from a mixed supply chain would need to track Notice 2026-15 closely because a taxpayer may rely on the rules provided in the notice to calculate the material assistance cost ratio for any Section 45Y or 48E qualified facility or energy storage technology the construction of which begins after Dec. 31, 2025, until 60 days after the publication of the forthcoming safe harbor tables. If the deal's beginning-of-construction date falls near this threshold, or if the forthcoming safe harbor tables are published mid-negotiation, the tax equity investor's counsel would need to immediately reassess whether the project's FEOC compliance representations remain accurate, potentially triggering renegotiation of indemnity scope or additional documentation requirements before funding.

**[Inference]** Given the pace of FEOC-related guidance issuance through 2026, deals with signing-to-funding gaps of several months carry elevated risk of guidance changes occurring mid-transaction, making real-time tracking (rather than periodic review) a practical necessity rather than a best practice.
