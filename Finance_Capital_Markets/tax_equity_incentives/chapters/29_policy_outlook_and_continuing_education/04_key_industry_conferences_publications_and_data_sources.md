## Key Industry Conferences, Publications, and Data Sources


### Overview

Staying current in tax equity and project finance requires sustained engagement with a specific ecosystem of industry conferences, trade publications, data providers, and professional networks. Unlike broader corporate finance, this is a relatively concentrated professional community where a manageable set of recurring events and publications covers the vast majority of market-moving information. This topic surveys the major conferences, publications, law firm resources, and data sources that practitioners rely on to stay current on deal terms, policy developments, and market pricing.

### Key Points

- The tax equity and project finance information ecosystem spans conferences (deal-making and networking), trade publications (news and analysis), law firm client alerts (technical/legal interpretation), and specialty data providers (deal terms, pricing benchmarks)
- Given the field's concentrated professional community, in-person conference attendance often provides information and relationship value that published sources cannot fully replicate
- No single source provides comprehensive coverage; practitioners typically triangulate across several categories simultaneously
- **[Unverified]** Specific attendance figures, exact conference dates, and subscription pricing for the sources below should be confirmed directly with organizers/publishers, as these details change year to year and were not independently verified for this response.

### Industry Conferences

**1. Infocast Conferences**

Infocast runs a series of specialty conferences covering renewable energy finance broadly, including dedicated tax equity and project finance tracks. These events are widely attended by sponsors, tax equity investors, law firms, and accounting firms, and are commonly cited as key venues for deal networking and market intelligence gathering.

**2. American Council on Renewable Energy (ACORE) Events**

ACORE hosts finance-focused convenings that bring together renewable energy developers, investors, and policymakers, often featuring direct engagement with regulatory and legislative stakeholders — valuable given the current environment's active legislative and rulemaking pace.

**3. Solar and Wind Industry-Specific Conferences**

- **RE+ (formerly Solar Power International/SPI)** — the largest US solar industry trade event, covering technology, policy, and finance tracks
- **American Clean Power Association (ACP) events** — cover wind, solar, and storage policy and finance, including dedicated finance and tax credit sessions

**4. Tax-Focused Conferences**

- **Novogradac conferences** — Novogradac is particularly known for coverage spanning multiple tax credit markets (renewable energy, LIHTC, historic tax credits, new markets tax credits), making their events valuable for practitioners tracking cross-credit-market structuring trends
- **Specialty tax equity structuring seminars** hosted by major law firms and accounting firms, often CLE/CPE-accredited

**5. Storage-Specific Events**

Given the rapid growth and evolving structuring practices for battery storage tax equity (a less mature deal market than solar/wind with less standardized terms), dedicated energy storage finance conferences have grown in relevance.

### Trade Publications and News Sources

**1. Specialty Tax and Energy Finance Publications**

- **Tax Notes** — provides same-day technical analysis of Treasury/IRS guidance, widely used by tax practitioners for guidance tracking (see related topic)
- **Novogradac Journal of Tax Credits** — dedicated coverage of tax equity markets across renewable energy and other tax credit categories
- **Project Finance International** and similar structured finance trade publications — cover broader project finance market trends, including tax equity as one component of the capital stack

**2. General Energy Industry Publications**

- **Utility Dive** — covers utility-scale renewable energy policy, deal activity, and legislative developments, frequently reporting on tax credit legislation as it moves through Congress
- **Reuters/Bloomberg energy and tax desks** — general business press coverage of major policy and market developments
- **Canary Media, Latitude Media** — clean energy-focused digital publications covering market and policy trends with an accessible, less technical framing than specialty tax publications

**3. Law Firm Client Alerts and Knowledge Hubs**

Major law firms with dedicated project finance and energy tax practices maintain regularly updated client alert systems and, in some cases, structured knowledge libraries tracking guidance chronologically. These are frequently the fastest source of technical analysis following a new notice or regulation, often published same-day or next-day. Firms active in this space typically maintain dedicated renewable energy and tax credit practice group pages with searchable alert archives.

**4. Accounting Firm Insights**

Big Four and specialty accounting firms (particularly those with substantial renewable energy tax practices) publish technical alerts and broader market outlook pieces, often with a more numbers-driven or modeling-oriented framing than law firm alerts.

### Data Sources and Market Intelligence

**1. Deal Pricing and Terms Benchmarking**

- **Specialty tax equity market surveys** — periodic surveys (sometimes conducted by law firms, banks, or industry associations) aggregating anonymized deal pricing (cents per ITC dollar, target investor IRRs) across recent transactions, used as pricing benchmarks in negotiations
- **[Inference]** Given the bilateral, privately negotiated nature of most tax equity deals, pricing data is generally less transparent and less real-time than public capital markets, making relationship-based information sharing (through counsel, conferences, and informal market contacts) an important supplement to any published survey data

**2. Government and Regulatory Data Sources**

- **IRS.gov** — primary source for all official guidance (see related topic on real-time guidance tracking)
- **Federal Register** — proposed and final regulations, comment periods
- **U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA)** — energy market data, generation capacity statistics, relevant for underwriting and market context
- **Department of Energy** — policy announcements and program data, particularly relevant given DOE's role in loan guarantee and demonstration programs that sometimes intersect with tax equity-financed projects

**3. Industry Association Data and Advocacy Resources**

- **American Clean Power Association (ACP)** — publishes market data, policy positions, and advocacy materials relevant to legislative risk monitoring
- **Solar Energy Industries Association (SEIA)** — solar-specific market data and policy tracking
- **ACORE** — cross-technology renewable finance policy resources

**4. Independent Research and Analysis**

- **Rhodium Group** — independent research organization frequently cited for quantitative analysis of policy impact on clean energy investment (e.g., estimates of investment at risk following OBBBA), useful for market-level context beyond individual deal terms
- **Think tank policy analysis** (e.g., Peter G. Peterson Foundation and similar fiscal policy research organizations) — useful for understanding the budgetary and fiscal policy context behind legislative changes

### Building a Personal Monitoring Stack

**Example practitioner routine:**

- **Daily**: scan IRS.gov newsroom and 1-2 law firm alert digests for new guidance
- **Weekly**: review Tax Notes and Utility Dive for policy/legislative developments
- **Monthly**: read Novogradac Journal of Tax Credits or similar specialty publication for deeper structuring analysis
- **Quarterly/Annually**: attend at least one major conference (Infocast, RE+, or ACP) for relationship maintenance and informal market pricing intelligence
- **As-needed**: consult independent research (Rhodium Group and similar) when assessing the market-wide impact of a major legislative or regulatory development

### Limitations of Published Sources

- **Pricing opacity**: because most tax equity deals are privately negotiated and not publicly disclosed in detail, published market surveys necessarily lag actual market conditions and may reflect only voluntarily reported data
- **Publication lag on technical analysis**: while law firm alerts are typically fast, the most authoritative technical analysis (addressing subtle interpretive questions) sometimes takes weeks to emerge as practitioners work through the guidance in practice
- **Conference information asymmetry**: **[Inference]** much of the most current pricing and deal-term information likely circulates informally at conferences and through direct relationships rather than through any published source, meaning practitioners who rely solely on written publications may lag those with strong conference and relationship networks
