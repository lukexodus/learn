## Applications of High Entropy Alloys


### Overview

Applications of high entropy alloys span structural, functional, and extreme-environment domains, leveraging the property combinations established in the preceding chapter items: cryogenic toughness in FCC systems, high-temperature strength in refractory BCC systems, and precipitate-strengthened performance in engineered multi-phase compositions. This item surveys the primary application sectors, the specific HEA properties each sector exploits, and the maturity gap between laboratory demonstration and qualified commercial deployment.

### Cryogenic and Low-Temperature Structural Applications

**Key Points**

- FCC Cantor-type HEAs, given their unusual property combination of increasing strength, ductility, and fracture toughness with decreasing temperature (covered in the mechanical properties item), are of particular interest for cryogenic structural applications such as liquefied natural gas (LNG) storage/transport infrastructure, cryogenic pressure vessels, and space-launch cryogenic propellant system components
- This application space directly targets the deformation-twinning-enabled toughness mechanism, since conventional structural steels used in cryogenic service (e.g., austenitic stainless steels, 9% Ni steel) are themselves selected specifically to avoid ductile-to-brittle transition, making FCC HEAs a potentially competitive alternative class for the same service niche
- Qualification for actual cryogenic structural service requires extensive standardized mechanical testing, long-term property stability data, and weldability/joining characterization that remain less mature for HEAs than for the incumbent qualified cryogenic steel grades, representing the primary barrier to near-term adoption in this sector [Inference: the specific pace of HEA qualification for cryogenic structural codes and standards varies by jurisdiction and application, and is not yet uniformly established]

### High-Temperature Structural and Aerospace Applications

**Key Points**

- Refractory HEAs (RHEAs), covered in detail in the preceding chapter item, are targeted at high-temperature structural applications exceeding the practical service temperature ceiling of conventional Ni-based superalloys, with aerospace propulsion (turbine components, hypersonic vehicle structures) and energy-sector high-temperature components (e.g., next-generation power-cycle hardware) cited as primary target application areas
- The room-temperature ductility and oxidation-resistance challenges discussed in the RHEA item remain the primary barriers to structural qualification in this sector, meaning current RHEA application activity is concentrated substantially in research, prototype, and early demonstration-component stages rather than qualified production hardware
- Coating and environmental-barrier integration (adapting Ni-superalloy and ceramic-matrix-composite coating technology) is generally viewed as a necessary complementary strategy for enabling RHEA structural use in oxidizing high-temperature service environments, rather than relying on intrinsic RHEA oxidation resistance alone
- Precipitate-strengthened, second-generation HEA/MPEA compositions (combining a ductile matrix with strengthening precipitates, as covered in the microstructure and mechanical properties items) represent a parallel high-temperature structural development pathway aimed at achieving a more favorable strength-ductility-oxidation balance than base refractory compositions alone

### Wear-Resistant and Tribological Coatings

**Key Points**

- HEA-based coatings, deposited via techniques such as laser cladding, thermal spray, magnetron sputtering, and arc-melting-based surface alloying, are among the more mature near-term application areas for HEA technology, since coating applications can exploit favorable HEA hardness and wear properties without requiring the full bulk-structural mechanical qualification demanded of load-bearing structural components
- FCC and BCC HEA coating compositions have shown favorable wear resistance, hardness, and in some cases corrosion resistance relative to conventional coating alloys (e.g., certain Co- or Ni-based hardfacing alloys, stainless steel coatings), motivating interest in tooling, wear-resistant machine components, and surface-protection applications
- Laser cladding and additive-manufacturing-based coating deposition allow relatively straightforward compositional flexibility for tailoring coating chemistry to a specific substrate and wear/corrosion environment, an attractive feature for application-specific coating optimization compared to more constrained conventional coating alloy selection
- High-entropy nitride, carbide, and oxide coatings (extending the compositional-complexity concept into hard ceramic coating systems, related to the high-entropy ceramics research direction) represent a further actively developing coating application subclass targeting extreme hardness and thermal stability

### Corrosion-Resistant Applications

**Key Points**

- Several HEA compositions, particularly those incorporating Cr, Mo, and Ni in combination (drawing on corrosion-resistance design principles familiar from conventional stainless steels and Ni-based corrosion-resistant alloys), have demonstrated favorable corrosion resistance in aqueous and chloride-containing environments, motivating interest in marine, chemical-processing, and other corrosive-service applications
- The severe lattice distortion and potential short-range ordering effects characteristic of HEAs can influence passive-film formation and stability in ways that differ from conventional alloys, an active area of electrochemical characterization research specific to HEA corrosion behavior
- As with structural applications, corrosion-resistant HEA application maturity varies substantially by specific composition and target service environment, with some compositions demonstrating properties competitive with established corrosion-resistant alloys in laboratory testing while broader field-service validation data remains comparatively limited relative to long-established conventional corrosion-resistant alloy families

### Irradiation-Resistant and Nuclear Applications

**Key Points**

- HEAs are of specific research interest for nuclear reactor structural and cladding applications given evidence that the compositionally complex, severely lattice-distorted structure may confer enhanced radiation damage tolerance relative to conventional alloys, through mechanisms proposed to include enhanced point-defect recombination and reduced defect-cluster mobility in the locally disordered lattice environment
- This radiation-tolerance research connects directly to the sluggish diffusion effect discussed in the concept and design principles item, since reduced defect/solute mobility under irradiation would be mechanistically consistent with (though not definitively proven by) the broader sluggish-diffusion hypothesis
- Nuclear structural material qualification carries exceptionally stringent, long-duration testing and regulatory requirements (including extended in-reactor irradiation testing) that place HEA nuclear applications firmly in the research and long-term-development category at present, well short of qualified deployment status [Unverified: the extent and mechanistic basis of HEA irradiation tolerance remains an active research question, and specific compositions' performance varies; nuclear-grade qualification is a multi-decade-scale process for any new structural material class]
- Refractory and FCC HEA compositions are both represented in nuclear-relevant HEA research, reflecting the different structural roles (high-temperature core components vs. lower-temperature structural/cladding applications) within reactor design that might eventually draw on HEA radiation-tolerance properties

### Functional and Non-Structural Applications

**Key Points**

- Hydrogen storage: certain BCC HEA compositions have been investigated as hydrogen-absorbing materials, drawing conceptually on the interstitial metal hydride principles covered in the earlier hydrogen storage materials chapter item, but applying them within a compositionally complex multi-principal-element BCC matrix rather than a conventional AB-type intermetallic, an active area of exploratory research rather than established commercial hydrogen-storage technology
- Catalytic applications: HEA and high-entropy nanoparticle compositions have been explored as electrocatalysts (connecting to the catalyst materials for energy conversion chapter item), motivated by the hypothesis that compositional complexity may enable more favorable, tunable adsorbate binding energies than single- or dual-element catalyst surfaces, and potentially help address the scaling-relation limitations discussed in that earlier item, though this remains a comparatively young and actively developing research direction relative to established PGM and PGM-free catalyst systems [Unverified: HEA/high-entropy-nanoparticle catalyst performance advantages over optimized conventional catalysts are reported unevenly across specific reactions and studies, and the field's overall maturity relative to established catalyst systems should not be overstated]
- Soft and hard magnetic HEAs: certain compositions incorporating ferromagnetic elements (Fe, Co, Ni) have been explored for tunable soft-magnetic or, less commonly, hard-magnetic functional applications, exploiting compositional flexibility to tune magnetic properties (saturation magnetization, coercivity, magnetostriction) in ways not straightforwardly achievable in conventional magnetic alloy systems
- Thermoelectric HEAs: the same compositional complexity that reduces thermal conductivity via enhanced phonon scattering (discussed in the mechanical properties item's physical-property interplay section) is of direct interest for thermoelectric applications, where low thermal conductivity is a key figure-of-merit driver, motivating exploratory HEA thermoelectric materials research

### Maturity and Commercialization Landscape

**Key Points**

- Application maturity across the HEA field is highly uneven: coating and surface-modification applications are comparatively closer to practical/commercial use given their lower qualification burden, while bulk structural applications (cryogenic, high-temperature aerospace, nuclear) remain substantially in the research, prototyping, and early qualification stages
- The general pattern across HEA application sectors is that laboratory-demonstrated property advantages consistently outpace the long-duration, standardized qualification testing (mechanical, corrosion, fatigue, weldability, long-term thermal/microstructural stability) required before adoption in safety-critical or long-service-life structural applications, a maturity gap common to essentially all genuinely novel structural alloy classes rather than unique to HEAs specifically
- Cost and manufacturability (particularly for refractory-element-rich or otherwise expensive-constituent HEA compositions) remain significant practical adoption barriers independent of demonstrated property performance, reinforcing the cost/criticality-constrained design considerations discussed in the complex concentrated alloy design item
- Given the field's rapid pace of publication and the wide range of application-specific maturity levels, current application status for any specific HEA composition or sector should be verified against recent literature and industry sources rather than treated as fixed, since the transition from research to qualified/commercial deployment status can change meaningfully over a period of a few years [Unverified: application-readiness status across HEA sectors evolves continuously and specific current-state claims should be checked against up-to-date sources]
