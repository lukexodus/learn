## Contractor Prequalification and Selection

### Overview

Contractor prequalification and selection is the systematic process by which an owner/operator organization evaluates, screens, and selects contractors before allowing them to perform work at a facility, particularly work involving covered processes under Process Safety Management (PSM) programs. Under OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119(h), employers must obtain and evaluate information regarding the contract employer's safety performance and programs when selecting a contractor to perform maintenance, repair, turnaround, major renovation, or specialty work on or adjacent to a covered process.

The objective is to ensure contract workers achieve the same level of process safety awareness and competence as host employer employees, reducing the risk of incidents caused by unfamiliarity with process hazards, procedures, or emergency response requirements.

### Regulatory Basis

**Key Points**

- **OSHA PSM Standard 1910.119(h)(1)**: Applies to contractors performing maintenance, repair, turnaround, major renovation, or specialty work on or near a covered process; does not apply to contractors providing incidental services that do not influence process safety (e.g., janitorial, food, laundry).
- **1910.119(h)(2)(i)**: The host employer must obtain and evaluate information regarding the contract employer's safety performance and programs when selecting a contractor.
- **EPA Risk Management Program (40 CFR 68.87)**: Contains parallel contractor requirements for facilities subject to RMP.
- **OSHA General Duty Clause / Multi-Employer Worksite Doctrine**: Host and contract employers can share liability for hazards affecting contract workers, reinforcing the need for rigorous prequalification.

### Objectives of Prequalification

- Verify the contractor's technical competence to perform the specific scope of work safely.
- Confirm the contractor maintains an acceptable safety record (injury/illness rates, incident history).
- Ensure the contractor has documented safety and health programs consistent with host expectations (e.g., hazard communication, LOTO, confined space, hot work, fall protection).
- Confirm adequate insurance, licensing, certifications, and regulatory compliance history.
- Reduce the host employer's exposure to incidents, regulatory citations, and multi-employer liability.
- Establish a pool of pre-vetted contractors to reduce selection time for urgent or turnaround work.

### Data Points Collected During Prequalification

**Key Points**

1. **Injury and Illness Statistics**
   - Total Recordable Incident Rate (TRIR)
   - Days Away, Restricted, or Transferred (DART) rate
   - Experience Modification Rate (EMR), typically requiring $EMR < 1.0$
   - OSHA 300/300A logs for the past 3–5 years
2. **Regulatory Compliance History**
   - OSHA citation history (via OSHA's public inspection database)
   - Any Notices of Violation, willful/serious citations, or fatalities
3. **Safety Management Systems**
   - Written safety programs: LOTO, confined space entry, hot work, fall protection, respiratory protection, PSM/RMP-specific training
   - Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) / Job Safety Analysis (JSA) procedures
   - Emergency response and evacuation procedures
   - Training and certification records (e.g., OSHA 10/30, competent person designations, welding certifications, crane operator certifications)
4. **Insurance and Financial Verification**
   - General liability, workers' compensation, and umbrella insurance certificates
   - Bonding capacity for large-scope projects
   - Financial stability (for long-duration or high-value contracts)
5. **References and Past Performance**
   - Prior client references, particularly from similar high-hazard facilities
   - Documented incident history with prior clients

### Common Prequalification Scoring Model

A weighted scoring matrix is often used to rank contractors objectively:

| Criterion | Weight | Example Scoring Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TRIR / DART rates | 25% | Compared against industry BLS benchmark |
| EMR | 20% | Below 1.0 preferred; penalize >1.2 |
| OSHA citation history | 20% | Deduct for serious/willful citations in past 3 yrs |
| Written safety program quality | 15% | Completeness against host checklist |
| Training/certification currency | 10% | % of workforce with valid certifications |
| References/past performance | 10% | Client feedback score |

A composite score is calculated as:

$$S = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i \times s_i$$

where $w_i$ is the weight of criterion $i$ and $s_i$ is the normalized score (0–100) for that criterion. Facilities typically set a minimum threshold (e.g., $S \geq 70$) for inclusion on the Approved Contractor List (ACL).

### Selection Criteria Beyond Safety Performance

- **Technical competence**: demonstrated experience with the specific type of work (e.g., pressure vessel repair, instrumentation calibration, rotating equipment).
- **Familiarity with process hazards**: prior experience in similar PSM-covered environments (refining, chemical processing, oil & gas).
- **Availability and capacity**: workforce size and equipment availability relative to project scope and schedule.
- **Cultural fit**: willingness to comply with host site safety culture, stop-work-authority policies, and behavior-based safety programs.
- **Subcontractor management**: contractor's own process for prequalifying and overseeing their subcontractors (tiered contractor risk).

### Host Employer Responsibilities Under 1910.119(h)(2)

**Key Points**

- Inform contract employers of known potential fire, explosion, or toxic release hazards related to the contractor's work and the process.
- Explain the applicable provisions of the emergency action plan.
- Develop and implement safe work practices to control the entrance, presence, and exit of contract employers and employees in covered process areas.
- Periodically evaluate the performance of contract employers in fulfilling their obligations (documented, e.g., annually or at project close-out).
- Maintain a contract employee injury/illness log related to the contractor's work in the covered process area.

### Contract Employer (Contractor) Responsibilities Under 1910.119(h)(3)

- Ensure contract employees are trained in the work practices necessary to perform their job safely.
- Ensure contract employees are instructed in known potential fire, explosion, or toxic release hazards and the applicable provisions of the emergency action plan.
- Document that each contract employee has received and understood the training required by the standard.
- Ensure contract employees follow the safety rules of the facility, including PPE requirements.
- Advise the host employer of any unique hazards presented by the contract employer's work, or of hazards found during the work that were not previously identified.

### Ongoing Contractor Performance Evaluation

Prequalification is not a one-time event; PSM requires periodic evaluation of contractor performance (1910.119(h)(2)(iii)). Common mechanisms include:

- Scorecards tracking near-misses, incidents, and audit findings per contract or per year.
- Post-job debriefs and lessons-learned sessions after turnarounds or major projects.
- Re-qualification cycles (typically annual) requiring updated TRIR/DART/EMR data and insurance certificates.
- Immediate suspension or removal from the ACL for serious safety violations, willful non-compliance, or a reportable incident attributable to contractor negligence.

### Common Pitfalls

- Relying solely on lagging indicators (TRIR/EMR) without evaluating leading indicators such as safety program maturity or training currency.
- Failing to differentiate prequalification requirements by risk tier (e.g., applying the same rigor to a low-risk vendor as to a turnaround contractor working inside a covered process).
- Not verifying subcontractor (second-tier) safety performance, creating blind spots in multi-tier contracting arrangements.
- Treating prequalification as a procurement/administrative function disconnected from EHS oversight. [Inference: this is a commonly cited industry finding, though the degree varies by organization.]
- Allowing expired certifications or insurance to lapse without triggering automatic ACL suspension.
