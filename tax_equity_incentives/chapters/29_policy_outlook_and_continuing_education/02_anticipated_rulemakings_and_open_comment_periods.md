## Anticipated Rulemakings and Open Comment Periods


### Overview

Beyond tracking guidance already issued, tax equity practitioners must maintain forward visibility into rulemakings that are announced but not yet finalized — proposed regulations pending comment and public hearing, notices that explicitly promise forthcoming proposed regulations, and statutory deadlines requiring Treasury to publish specific guidance (such as safe harbor tables) by a fixed date. This forward-looking tracking discipline differs from real-time guidance monitoring in that it focuses on the rulemaking pipeline itself — comment deadlines, hearing dates, and statutory publication deadlines — so that deal teams can anticipate when settled expectations may change and can participate in the rulemaking process where it affects their interests.

### Key Points

- Anticipated rulemakings fall into two categories: **notices signaling forthcoming proposed regulations** (interim guidance with an explicit promise of future rulemaking) and **proposed regulations already published** and open for comment before finalization
- Statutory deadlines embedded in OBBBA and prior legislation (e.g., a fixed date by which Treasury must publish specific safe harbor tables) are hard tracking dates that materially affect deal timing and structuring certainty
- Comment period participation is a legitimate avenue for sponsors, investors, and industry associations to influence final rule content, and tracking open comment periods allows organizations to decide whether to submit comments or monitor others' submissions
- Reliance rules attached to proposed regulations (whether taxpayers may rely on proposed rules before finalization) are a critical, deal-relevant detail buried within each rulemaking notice

### Categories of Anticipated Rulemaking

**1. Notices with an explicit promise of forthcoming proposed regulations**

Several recent notices explicitly commit Treasury and the IRS to future rulemaking while providing interim operative rules in the meantime. Notice 2026-15 provides that Treasury and IRS intend to propose regulations with respect to the definition of a PFE and the calculation of the material assistance cost ratio that taxpayers must use to determine whether there was material assistance from a PFE, and separately, that Treasury and IRS intend to propose regulations and other further guidance with respect to the definition of a PFE and the material assistance rules, including new safe harbor tables as authorized in the OBBB.

**2. Proposed regulations already published, open for comment**

Once published, proposed regulations move into a formal notice-and-comment rulemaking process under the Administrative Procedure Act, with a defined comment deadline and often a scheduled public hearing. For example, regarding the Section 45Z clean fuel production credit, Treasury and IRS published proposed regulations on February 6, 2026, and comments must be received by the Treasury Department and IRS by April 6, 2026, with a public hearing scheduled to be held on May 28, 2026, at 10 a.m. ET.

**3. Statutory deadlines for mandatory guidance publication**

Some guidance deadlines are not discretionary but are fixed by statute. Treasury plans proposed regulations and must publish PFE-specific safe harbor tables by Dec. 31, 2026, with comments for the associated proposed regulations due March 30, 2026 — meaning practitioners can calendar a hard external deadline (informed by statute) rather than relying solely on agency discretion.

### Why Tracking the Pipeline (Not Just Issued Guidance) Matters

**1. Deal timing around anticipated finalization**

If a statutory deadline requires safe harbor tables by a fixed date, sponsors and investors negotiating deals with funding dates near that deadline must build contractual flexibility into representations and indemnities, anticipating that operative rules may change between signing and funding.

**2. Reliance period expiration as a structuring trigger**

Interim notices typically specify that reliance is permitted only until a defined trigger event. The proposed regulations allow reliance thereon until final regulations are published in the Federal Register, provided that taxpayers follow them in their entirety and in a consistent manner — meaning a deal team must track not just whether proposed regulations exist, but the specific conditions (full and consistent application) required to preserve reliance.

**3. Comment period participation strategy**

Organizations with material exposure to a specific rulemaking may choose to:

- Submit formal comments directly or through counsel
- Participate in or monitor industry association joint comment letters
- Request to speak at a scheduled public hearing — for the Section 45Z regulations, requests to speak and outlines of topics to be discussed at the public hearing must be received by [a specified deadline], illustrating that hearing participation itself has a separate, earlier deadline than the comment submission deadline

**4. Iterative guidance layering**

Some guidance areas are cumulative and iterative rather than singular events — the energy community bonus credit guidance illustrates this pattern, where Notice 2026-39 includes lists of information that taxpayers may use to determine whether they meet certain requirements... as described in Notice 2023-29, Notice 2023-45, Notice 2023-47, Notice 2024-30, Notice 2024-48, and Notice 2025-31, with taxpayers permitted to rely on the entire chain of notices until the eventual proposed regulations are issued. Tracking this area requires maintaining the full chain of applicable notices, not just the most recent one.

### Practical Tracking Checklist for Deal Teams

- **Maintain a rulemaking calendar** listing: notice/reg citation, comment deadline, hearing date (if any), and any statutory publication deadline
- **Flag deals with funding dates within 6-12 months of an anticipated finalization date** for heightened monitoring
- **Confirm reliance conditions explicitly** — full and consistent application requirements are common and easy to overlook when relying on proposed regulations
- **Coordinate with tax counsel on comment letter strategy** where a specific rulemaking would materially affect deal economics or structuring flexibility
- **Cross-reference statutory deadlines** (such as the Section 7701(a)(52) requirement that Treasury publish safe harbor tables by Dec. 31, 2026) against the Code itself, since these deadlines carry more certainty than discretionary agency timelines

### Example: Structuring Around an Open Comment Period

A sponsor negotiating a partnership flip for a facility with mixed-origin components would need to account for the fact that Treasury plans proposed regulations and must publish PFE-specific safe harbor tables by Dec. 31, 2026, while comments for those proposed regulations are due March 30, 2026. If the deal's funding date falls in Q4 2026, the LLC Agreement's FEOC compliance representations should explicitly address the possibility that the safe harbor tables in effect at signing may be superseded before funding, with a mechanism (such as a bring-down certificate referencing the then-current safe harbor tables) rather than a static reference to the interim tables alone.
