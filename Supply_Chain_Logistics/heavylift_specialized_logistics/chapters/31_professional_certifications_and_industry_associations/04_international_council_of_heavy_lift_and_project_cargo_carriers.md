## International Council of Heavy Lift and Project Cargo Carriers


### Overview

The International Council of Heavy Lift and Project Cargo Carriers — widely known as the "Heavy Lift Club" (HLC) — is a trade organisation representing ocean carriers engaged in the international transport of heavy lift and project cargo. Unlike NCCCO (individual personnel certification) or ICHCA and SC&RA (broader cross-sector trade associations), the Heavy Lift Club is specifically constituted as a carrier-membership body for the ocean-going heavy lift and project cargo shipping segment, with membership restricted to qualifying ocean carriers rather than open to the full range of industry stakeholders.

---

### Founding and Purpose

**Key Points**

- In May 2009, the International Council of Heavy Lift and Project Cargo Carriers (the "Heavy Lift Club") was established in order to create a forum for exchanging ideas on industry issues that concern carriers operating in the heavy lift and project cargo sector.
- The Council was founded at a meeting held prior to the start of the Breakbulk Europe Conference in Antwerp, where ocean carriers involved with the international transport of project and heavy lift cargoes announced the founding of the organisation.
- The creation of the Heavy Lift Club reflected concern by major players in this specialised area that technical, operational and safety considerations relating to their market were not always properly understood — reflecting a founding motivation centered on industry visibility and technical understanding, distinct from a purely commercial or lobbying rationale.
- Jan (Boje) Steffens, from Rickmers-Linie, was selected as the group's founding chairman. Steffens stated that heavy lift and project cargo carriers are crucial to world infrastructure, which affects everyone, and that the work of heavy lift and project cargo carriers is not only important, it is specialised — highlighting that there are technical, operational and safety considerations in the sector, along with important challenges, that are not always considered or understood by the broader shipping industry or public.
- The Heavy Lift Club provides a forum for exchanging ideas on industry matters that are of interest and concern to heavy lift and project carriers — its core function is deliberately framed as a forum/discussion body rather than a certification, regulatory, or standard-setting body.

---

### Scope of Issues Addressed

**Key Points**

- Issues of importance and concern to the Heavy Lift Club include education, technological innovation, the environment, security, and the awareness of considerations involved with the marine transport of heavy lift and project cargoes.
- The organisation's later commentary (via its chairman) has specifically addressed how the growth of containerisation in global trade has altered the face of ports and dock-side labour to a point where safety can become compromised for break-bulk and heavy-lift shippers — noting that while many port authorities and investors have prioritised container terminal investment, heavy lift facilities have in many cases been comparatively neglected.
- A related safety concern raised by the Club's chairman is that, in many parts of the world, a lack of suitably qualified stevedores, combined with the growing use of labour-pool workers who are more familiar with container operations than heavy-lift cargo handling, has had a negative consequence on the safety of heavy-lift cargo handling specifically.
- The Club's founding leadership stressed the importance of technology and of the heavy-lift sector in general to global infrastructure, framing the organisation's advocacy scope as extending beyond narrow commercial interest to broader public-infrastructure relevance.

---

### Membership Structure

**Key Points**

- HLC membership is open to ocean carriers who are routinely engaged in the international transport of heavy lift and project cargoes through the use of a long-term controlled fleet of self-sustained heavy lift vessels — a specific, restrictive membership criterion that limits eligibility to carriers with dedicated, self-owned/controlled heavy-lift tonnage, rather than freight forwarders, brokers, or occasional charterers of heavy-lift capacity.
- The Heavy Lift Club has a non-rate discussion agreement filed with the U.S. Federal Maritime Commission — indicating the organisation operates under a formal regulatory filing that permits carrier members to discuss non-rate matters collectively (such as safety, technical, and operational issues) while remaining compliant with U.S. antitrust/competition regulatory requirements governing carrier conferences and discussion agreements.
- Founding members announced as members of the Heavy Lift Club, pending Federal Maritime Commission approval, included: Australia Asia Line; BigLift Shipping BV; Beluga Chartering GmbH; BBC Chartering & Logistic GmbH & Co KG; Chipolbrok; Clipper Projects A/S; Conti-Lines; Hyundai Merchant Marine; Intermarine, LLC (representing Industrial Maritime Carriers, LLC); K/S Combi Lift; Nordana; Rickmers-Linie GmbH & Cie KG; Scan-Scott; Scan-Trans; and Universal Africa Lines Ltd.
- [Inference: several of these founding member companies have since undergone ownership changes, mergers, or rebranding in the years since 2009 (for example, BigLift Shipping is now part of the Jumbo-SAL-Intermarine/JSI Alliance structure referenced elsewhere in this course material) — current HLC membership composition should be verified against the organisation's present-day member list rather than assumed to match this 2009 founding roster.]

### Relationship to Industry Contractual Standards (BIMCO)

Although the Heavy Lift Club itself functions primarily as a discussion forum rather than a standards-drafting body, the broader heavy lift/project cargo sector it represents relies heavily on standard charter party forms developed by BIMCO (the Baltic and International Maritime Council) — and HLC member companies have been directly involved in drafting these industry-standard contracts.

**Key Points**

- Apart from the practical considerations that arise in relation to heavy lift cargoes, there are also diverse contractual needs in the heavy lift sector that need to be met; BIMCO has produced two principal heavy lift charter party forms, both classed as special voyage charter parties for the heavy lift trade, with significant differences in their application and use.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[BIMCO Heavy Lift Charter Party Forms (svg_diagram)] --> B[HEAVYCON 2007]
    A --> C[HEAVYLIFTVOY 2009]
    B --> B1[Knock-for-knock liability regime]
    B --> B2[Semi-submersible / super-heavy-lift float-on-float-off]
    B --> B3[Cargoes almost always on deck, usually sole cargo]
    B --> B4[Accompanied by HEAVYCONBILL]
    C --> C1[Hague / Hague-Visby Rules liability regime]
    C --> C2[Mid-sized lift-on/lift-off and roll-on/roll-off sector]
    C --> C3[Multiple shipments, on and below deck]
    C --> C4[Free-in/liner-in hook loading; free-out/liner-out hook discharge]
    C --> C5[Accompanied by HEAVYLIFTVOYBILL]
```

**Key Points**

- **HEAVYCON 2007**: BIMCO's HEAVYCON 2007 voyage charterparty form is a re-issued and revised version of the original 1985 form and is accompanied by its own bill of lading (HEAVYCONBILL). It is a knock-for-knock contract designed primarily for use by semi-submersible vessels in the super-heavy lift (float-on/float-off) market, where cargoes are almost always loaded on deck and are usually sole cargoes; under a knock-for-knock contract, each contracting party bears responsibility for its own personnel and property without recourse to its counterparty, irrespective of fault. HEAVYCON's terms are flexible, covering a variety of loading and discharging methods as well as single or multiple loading and discharging ports, though it is considered less appropriate — though still used to some extent — in the midsized lift-on/lift-off and roll-on/roll-off sector. HEAVYCON was originally adopted by BIMCO's Documentary Committee at its meeting in Copenhagen in November 1985, and the updated HEAVYCON 2007 version was adopted at BIMCO's Documentary Committee meeting in Copenhagen in November 2007, developed by a team of heavylift specialists including representatives of BigLift Shipping, Schiffahrtskontor Altes Land, Fairmount, Heerema, Larsen & Partners, and the Standard Club.
- **HEAVYLIFTVOY**: HEAVYLIFTVOY is a voyage charter party for the mid-sized heavy lift sector carrying specialist cargo, providing for free-in or liner-in hook terms for loading and free-out or liner-out hook terms for discharging. Unlike HEAVYCON, HEAVYLIFTVOY operates under the conventional cargo liability regime of the Hague-Visby Rules — which impose on the carrier an obligation to exercise due diligence to provide a seaworthy vessel and to properly carry, keep, and care for the cargo — and is designed for the carriage of multiple shipments both above and below deck, accompanied by its own bill of lading, HEAVYLIFTVOYBILL. HEAVYLIFTVOY was developed in response to demand from heavy lift operators in the mid-sized lift-on/lift-off and roll-on/roll-off sector for a dedicated contract, replacing prior reliance on amended liner booking notes, which was not ideal given the specialised nature of the trade; it was originally developed from BIMCO's CONLINEBOOKING Note, adopted by BIMCO's Documentary Committee in mid-2007 (with the current edition dated 2009), and drafted by representatives from BigLift Shipping, Schiffahrtskontor Altes Land (SAL) Shipping, Jumbo Shipping, Liebherr Werk, the Standard Club, and Larsen & Partners.
- Notably, several of the same companies involved in drafting the BIMCO HEAVYCON and HEAVYLIFTVOY forms (BigLift Shipping, Jumbo Shipping, SAL) are among, or closely related to, the founding member companies of the Heavy Lift Club — illustrating that the sector's leading carriers have engaged in parallel efforts across both discussion-forum (HLC) and contractual-standardisation (BIMCO) initiatives to professionalise the heavy lift shipping trade.

### Relevance to Heavy-Lift and Specialized Logistics Practice

**Key Points**

- The Heavy Lift Club's membership restriction to carriers operating a long-term controlled fleet of self-sustained heavy lift vessels means it functions as a peer forum among asset-owning heavy-lift shipping lines specifically — distinguishing it from broader cross-sector bodies like SC&RA (land-based specialized transport/rigging, open to a wider range of company types) and ICHCA (all-mode cargo handling, broader membership base) covered elsewhere in this chapter.
- The BIMCO HEAVYCON and HEAVYLIFTVOY charter party forms remain foundational contractual instruments for the sector, and understanding which form applies to a given transaction — and its associated liability regime (knock-for-knock vs. Hague/Hague-Visby Rules) — is a practical necessity for anyone structuring or reviewing heavy-lift ocean charter arrangements.
- **Behavior may vary**: [Behavioral disclaimer] The Heavy Lift Club's current membership roster, governance structure, and active issue priorities are likely to have evolved since the organisation's 2009 founding and the mid-2010s commentary cited here; the specific BIMCO contract forms (HEAVYCON, HEAVYLIFTVOY) may also have been revised since the editions described. Both should be verified against current primary sources (the Heavy Lift Club and BIMCO respectively) before being relied upon for commercial or legal purposes.
