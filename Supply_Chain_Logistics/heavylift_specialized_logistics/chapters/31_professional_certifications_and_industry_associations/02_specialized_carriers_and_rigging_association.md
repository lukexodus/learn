## Specialized Carriers and Rigging Association


### Overview

The Specialized Carriers & Rigging Association (SC&RA) is an international trade association of more than 1,400 members from 46 nations, whose members are involved in specialized transportation, machinery moving and erecting, industrial maintenance, millwrighting, crane and rigging operations, manufacturing, and rental. SC&RA is the principal industry association serving the heavy-lift and specialized transport sector, functioning as an advocacy, education, and networking body rather than a personnel-certification body (in contrast to NCCCO, covered separately in this chapter) — its role is organizational and industry-wide rather than individual-credentialing.

---

### Mission and Core Functions

**Key Points**

- SC&RA's mission is simple: the organization advocates, educates, and provides networking opportunities that help industry stakeholders operate safely, legally, and profitably around the world.
- SC&RA helps members run more efficient and safer businesses by monitoring and affecting pending legislation and regulatory policies at the state and national levels — and, per the organization's own broader framing, also at the local level, extending its advocacy scope across all three levels of government.
- The organization pursues its mission by researching and reporting on safety concerns and best business practices, and by providing four yearly forums where these and other relevant member issues can be discussed and advanced.
- A related, more formal mission statement used in some SC&RA materials describes the organization as committed to providing the unique information and any other lawful activities members need to safely, legally, and profitably transport, lift, and erect oversize and overweight items — a framing that emphasizes the specific technical/operational core of the industry (oversize/overweight load handling) that unites SC&RA's diverse membership base.
- The stated overarching goal is to pursue improved safety, efficiency, productivity, and profitability for the specialized transportation, crane, and rigging industry as a whole.

---

### Membership Composition

**Key Points**

- SC&RA's membership spans six principal industry sectors: Specialized Transportation, Machinery Moving and Erecting, Crane and Rigging Operations, Industrial Maintenance, Millwrighting, and Manufacturing and Rental.
- More than 1,400 member companies across 46 nations operating in crane and rigging operations, specialized transportation, machinery moving and erecting, industrial maintenance, equipment rental, and supporting industries use SC&RA membership to protect their industry, partner with other member businesses, and realize cost savings each year.
- Membership has grown substantially over the organization's history: from 200 member companies by 1966, to 300 by 1968, crossing the 800-member mark by 1995, and reaching more than 1,400 member companies across 46 nations at the 75-year milestone (marked in 2022/2023) — reflecting sustained, multi-decade growth in both scale and international reach.
- Individual notable members illustrate the practical value proposition of SC&RA membership for operating companies: when Alan Barnhart and his brother Eric took over Barnhart Crane & Rigging from their parents in 1986, they joined SC&RA specifically as young business owners of a growing crane and rigging company seeking an industry advocate on legislation and regulations, as well as a way to keep up with the latest safety and best business practices; Barnhart subsequently grew from a handful of branches to over 50 locations across the United States.

**Key Points**

- SC&RA was formed in 1947 as laws and regulations specifically affecting the transport of specialized loads started to change; the organization began as a segment of the Local Cartage National Conference (LCNC) of the American Trucking Associations (ATA), created to accommodate the segment of the transportation industry affected by these regulatory developments.
- In December 1948, the Heavy Haulers, Machinery Movers and Erectors Section of LCNC was formally established, supported by eight original member companies, one of which was Dawes Rigging & Crane Rental, Inc.; the new Heavy-Specialized Conference of the ATA held its first official meeting on December 6 at the Mayflower Hotel in Washington, D.C., with 75 people in attendance.
- In 1981, the organization adopted its current, more streamlined name: the Specialized Carriers and Rigging Association.
- SC&RA marked its 75th anniversary in 2022–2023, spotlighting founding members, milestones, and achievements throughout the anniversary year — including a retrospective conversation with representatives of two of the original eight founding member companies.

### Governance Structure

**Key Points**

- SC&RA is governed by a Board of Directors and four groups, each of which reports to the Board regarding the work of their respective group.
- All Board and committee members are volunteers drawn from SC&RA member companies, bringing valuable industry knowledge — including insight into how regulatory decisions might impact member companies and current issues facing the membership.
- Each of the four groups maintains its own Governing and Nominating Committee, alongside other sub-committees as needed; Governing Committees are charged with identifying issues and recommending actions to benefit SC&RA members, as well as governing the actions of the committees within their respective groups. Committees are comprised of elected members from their respective industry sector.

### Programs, Events, and Recognition

**Key Points**

- In addition to advocacy and education, SC&RA hosts an annual conference that includes the Job of the Year (JOY) awards, recognizing outstanding projects across eight categories, including the prestigious hauling and rigging award categories — a significant industry recognition mechanism that showcases and benchmarks exceptional heavy-lift and specialized-transport project execution.
- SC&RA convenes committees on specific operational issues, such as its Permit Policy Committee, which meets to discuss challenges and set strategic direction on oversize/overweight transport permitting — an area of direct, ongoing relevance to heavy-haul logistics operators navigating varying state and jurisdictional permit regimes.
- The association runs a Future Leaders Forum (referenced for 2026), aimed at accelerating growth, expanding professional networks, and sharpening the decision-making skills of the next generation of industry leaders — reflecting an active succession-planning and workforce-development function alongside SC&RA's core advocacy role.
- SC&RA produces industry white papers examining the public benefits contributed by the specialized transportation, crane, and rigging industries, highlighting the essential roles these industries play in the safe and efficient movement of commercial goods and services globally — used as an external-facing advocacy and public-awareness tool.
- SC&RA maintains sponsor tiers (e.g., Patron Level, Friend Level) with member companies across crane and rigging, transportation, equipment, and supporting-service sectors providing financial support for association activities and events, such as the annual Crane & Rigging Workshop.

---

### Affiliated Organization: SC&RF (Specialized Carriers & Rigging Foundation)

**Key Points**

- The SC&RF (Specialized Carriers & Rigging Foundation) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization associated with SC&RA, wholly supported by company and individual financial contributions, and dedicated to supporting workforce development, providing educational assistance, and conducting industry-specific research to advance growth and opportunities within the crane, rigging, and specialized transport industry.
- Since its inception, SC&RF has invested nearly one million dollars in workforce development and education, and over half a million dollars in industry-specific research, benefiting SC&RA member companies and providing an advocacy platform for SC&RA.
- SC&RF's Educational Assistance committee oversees an awards program and the Partners In Education program, including a Collegiate Scholarship, a Vo/Tech Scholarship, and a Company Training Grant — directly addressing workforce pipeline development for the crane, rigging, and specialized-transport sector.
- SC&RF's Board of Directors and committees, like SC&RA's own governance structure, are comprised of volunteer members, with the organization explicitly encouraging individuals to serve on committees or contribute financially to the Foundation's mission.

### Relevance to Heavy-Lift and Specialized Logistics Practice

**Key Points**

- Unlike NCCCO (covered separately in this chapter), which certifies individual personnel (crane operators, riggers, signalpersons, lift directors), SC&RA operates at the organizational/company level — its core value proposition is industry advocacy, regulatory monitoring, best-practice research, and professional networking for companies and their leadership, rather than individual worker credentialing.
- SC&RA's permitting-focused committee work is of direct practical relevance to heavy-haul transport planning, given that oversize/overweight load permitting varies significantly by jurisdiction and is a recurring operational complexity for the sector (a theme also touched on in this chapter's coverage of modularization and its transport-complexity implications).
- The Job of the Year (JOY) awards and annual conference function as an informal industry benchmark, giving heavy-lift and specialized-transport professionals visibility into leading project execution examples across categories including hauling and rigging.
