## Personality Models and the Big Five


### Overview

Personality refers to relatively stable individual differences in patterns of thoughts, feelings, and behaviors across situations and time. In I-O Psychology, personality models serve as a foundational framework for understanding, measuring, and predicting workplace attitudes, behaviors, and performance outcomes, particularly in personnel selection contexts.

**Key Points**

- The Five-Factor Model (Big Five) is the dominant, most empirically supported personality taxonomy in contemporary I-O research and practice
- Personality traits generally show weaker but more consistent predictive validity for job performance than cognitive ability, though specific traits predict specific outcomes particularly well
- Personality assessment in selection contexts raises distinctive concerns around faking/impression management not present in ability testing

### The Five-Factor Model (Big Five)

**Historical development**: The Big Five emerged from decades of lexical and factor-analytic research (building on early work by Gordon Allport and Raymond Cattell, with the modern consolidated model most associated with researchers including Lewis Goldberg, Paul Costa, and Robert McCrae from the 1980s onward) converging on five broad, relatively independent trait dimensions that consistently emerge across cultures, languages, and measurement instruments.

**The five dimensions** (commonly remembered via the acronym OCEAN):

1. **Openness to Experience**: Intellectual curiosity, creativity, preference for novelty and variety versus convention and routine
2. **Conscientiousness**: Organization, dependability, self-discipline, achievement-striving, and impulse control
3. **Extraversion**: Sociability, assertiveness, positive emotionality, and energy derived from social interaction
4. **Agreeableness**: Cooperation, trust, empathy, and concern for social harmony versus competitiveness and skepticism
5. **Neuroticism** (sometimes inverted and labeled **Emotional Stability**): Tendency toward negative emotions, anxiety, and emotional volatility versus calm and resilience

### Big Five and Job Performance: Meta-Analytic Evidence

**Conscientiousness** is the most consistent predictor of job performance across virtually all occupations, reflecting its direct relevance to reliability, effort, and goal-directed behavior. Meta-analytic research (building on foundational work by Barrick and Mount in the early 1990s) established this as among the most robust findings in personality-performance research.

The other four traits show more **job-specific** predictive patterns rather than universal validity:

- **Extraversion** predicts performance more strongly in occupations involving social interaction and influence (sales, management)
- **Emotional Stability** predicts performance across most jobs but particularly in high-stress or customer-facing roles
- **Agreeableness** predicts performance in roles requiring teamwork and cooperation, though can show more complex or even negative relationships in highly competitive individual-performance roles
- **Openness** predicts performance in roles requiring creativity, adaptability, and learning, and specifically predicts training success

**[Inference]** While conscientiousness's broad validity is one of the most replicated findings in I-O research, exact meta-analytic effect size estimates vary somewhat across studies depending on criterion measures used (supervisor ratings vs. objective performance) and correction methods applied, so specific numerical validity coefficients should be treated as approximate rather than fixed.

### Alternative and Complementary Personality Frameworks

**HEXACO Model**

- An alternative six-factor model adding **Honesty-Humility** as a distinct dimension (sincerity, fairness, greed-avoidance, modesty) not fully captured within the Big Five's Agreeableness dimension
- Gaining increasing research attention, particularly for predicting counterproductive work behaviors and ethical/integrity-related outcomes

**Dark Triad**

- A framework capturing socially aversive personality traits: **Machiavellianism** (manipulativeness, strategic self-interest), **narcissism** (grandiosity, entitlement, need for admiration), and **psychopathy** (callousness, impulsivity, lack of empathy)
- Increasingly studied in leadership and counterproductive work behavior research, examining both potential "dark side" career advantages (e.g., narcissistic confidence aiding perceived leadership emergence) and clear organizational risks (unethical behavior, toxic leadership)

**Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)**

- A widely popular but psychometrically contested typological framework (categorizing individuals into 16 discrete types based on four dichotomies) frequently used in organizational training and team-building contexts
- **[Unverified]** The MBTI's popularity in organizational practice substantially exceeds its standing within the academic I-O psychometric literature; numerous published critiques have raised concerns about its test-retest reliability (many respondents receive a different "type" upon retesting) and its forced-dichotomy structure (treating continuous traits as binary categories), and mainstream academic I-O psychology generally favors the Big Five/FFM as more empirically defensible, though the MBTI continues to be widely used in applied organizational development and coaching contexts regardless of this academic critique.

### Personality Measurement Approaches

- **Self-report inventories**: The dominant measurement method (e.g., NEO-PI-R, IPIP, HEXACO-PI-R), using Likert-scale items asking individuals to rate themselves on trait-relevant statements
- **Observer/other-ratings**: Ratings provided by supervisors, peers, or family members, sometimes used to supplement or validate self-report data, particularly useful for addressing self-report limitations
- **Forced-choice/ipsative formats**: Requiring respondents to choose between equally desirable-sounding statements (rather than rating each independently) to reduce faking susceptibility, at some cost to certain statistical properties (ipsative scores are not fully independent across dimensions)
- **Situational judgment tests incorporating personality-relevant content**: Presenting workplace scenarios and asking respondents to select preferred responses, implicitly tapping personality-relevant tendencies alongside judgment

### Faking and Social Desirability in Selection Contexts

- **Faking/impression management**: A well-documented concern in high-stakes selection settings, where applicants may present idealized rather than accurate self-descriptions, potentially inflating scores and reducing predictive validity
- **[Unverified]** The practical, real-world impact of faking on selection validity and hiring decisions remains genuinely debated in the literature; some research suggests faking has relatively modest impact on the rank-ordering of candidates (and thus actual hiring decisions) despite raising average scores, while other research emphasizes more substantial practical consequences, particularly for candidates near selection cutoffs; this represents an active area of disagreement among psychometricians rather than a settled empirical conclusion.
- **Mitigation approaches**: Forced-choice formats, warning statements about detection of inconsistent responding, contextualized item wording (asking about behavior "at work" specifically), and using personality as one component within a broader multi-method selection battery rather than the sole basis for decisions

### Illustrative Example

**Scenario**: A company is designing a selection system for a customer service manager role.

- **Trait relevance analysis**: Job analysis suggests the role requires high interpersonal engagement (favoring Extraversion), reliability in following escalation procedures (favoring Conscientiousness), and composure under complaint-handling pressure (favoring Emotional Stability)
- **Instrument selection**: A validated Big Five instrument (e.g., an IPIP-based measure) is selected over MBTI-style typological tools, given stronger psychometric and predictive validity evidence
- **Faking mitigation**: A forced-choice format is used given the high-stakes selection context, alongside contextualized items specifying "at work" behavior
- **Multi-method integration**: Personality results are combined with a structured interview and job knowledge test rather than used in isolation, consistent with best practice given personality's generally moderate (rather than singularly dominant) standalone predictive validity

### Conclusion

The Big Five/Five-Factor Model represents the field's dominant, most empirically validated personality framework, with Conscientiousness standing out as a broadly generalizable predictor of job performance and the remaining traits showing more occupation-specific predictive patterns. While personality assessment offers valuable incremental validity beyond cognitive ability testing, particularly for predicting counterproductive behaviors and job-specific fit, its use in high-stakes selection requires careful attention to faking susceptibility and typically performs best as one component within a multi-method assessment battery rather than a standalone selection tool.
